\section{Costs Against the Alternatives}\label{sec:cost}

Joining, updating and leaving a shared output each cost more bytes than
the same operation on a bare UTXO. What sharing buys is a different
placement of the cost: entry is nearly free in bulk, value moves
off-chain for free, a bulk exit costs about a bare spend, and a solo exit
costs about ten times one but is a single transaction with no timelock and
no counterparty. All figures in this and the following sections use one
model: script, witness and control block as measured, plus a $946\WU$
skeleton (fee input, change, tree output, one P2TR payout) and $172\WU$
per further payout, $\mathrm{vB} = \lceil \mathrm{WU}/4\rceil$. Figures
marked \emph{estimate} are built from measured sizes for scripts not yet
generated.

\paragraph{Exit.} Figure~\ref{fig:anatomy} decomposes exits. The FULL
exit at $D = 16$ is $1{,}020\vB$, or $921\vB$ with the fee taken from the
payout. The hypothetical native opcode, at $388\vB$, approximates the
floor for any design that verifies a proof in script: the proof itself,
about 10\,vB per level, is still published. A radix-2 transaction tree of the
same size, in which the exiter publishes $D$ transactions of about
$150\vB$ and a $111\vB$ claim (spec 11), needs $2{,}511\vB$; the model
flatters it by ignoring the leaf's
timelock and the separate fee of each of its 16 transactions. In bulk the
cost per leaf depends on who signs. With \tr{EXIT}$_K$ every leaf carries
its own path, so a second exit costs $665\vB$; the operator's sweep of
expired leases shares the path and costs $203$, $158$ and $147\vB$ per
leaf for 13, 51 and 206 leaves, within 1.4 times a bare spend, but only
for members who accepted a lease. The owner-side equivalent is the cohort
rewrite of \S\ref{sec:batching}, estimated at 353 to $177\vB$ per leaf
for 4 to 51 owners. The limit of 25 unconfirmed descendants counts
transactions, not leaves.

\paragraph{Entry and moving value.} \tr{JOIN}$_h$ walks only the levels
above its slot, so at $D = 16$ a 1{,}024-member graft costs $657\vB$,
under $1\vB$ per member, against $43\vB$ for one output in an on-chain
batch and about $154\vB$ for a channel opened in its own transaction
(\S\ref{sec:graft}). An on-chain \tr{UPDATE} is $1{,}073\vB$ and a
voucher redemption $1{,}642\vB$, so moving value by rewriting leaves would
cost ten times a bare spend per payment. Value moves off-chain instead:
in channel leaves as HTLC forwards (\S\ref{sec:channels}), by vouchers
that the issuer usually settles rather than the holder redeems
(\S\ref{sec:receive}), or by \tr{SPLIT}: for 1{,}000 members moving
value 1{,}000 times each, about $1$\,kvB for one graft against $111$\,MvB
as bare UTXOs (estimate). \tr{UPDATE} is maintenance, not payment.

\paragraph{Lifetime and latency.} Table~\ref{tab:lifetime} adds entry and
exit. The dense tree wins outright for a member who never exits alone or
who is swept, loses to Lightning and to a bare UTXO for a member who exits
alone, and still beats an Ark round tree there by a factor of 2.5; the
sparse profile is the most expensive design in every column. A LayerTree
exit has no presigned transaction to lose, no counterparty, no timelock
and no expiry. It loses on the solo exit, the on-chain per-leaf update,
the sparse profile and serialisation through one output.
