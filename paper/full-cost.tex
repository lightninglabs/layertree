\section{Costs Against the Alternatives}\label{sec:cost}

Joining, updating and leaving a shared output each cost more bytes than
the same operation on a bare UTXO. What sharing buys is a different
placement of the cost: entry is nearly free in bulk, value moves
off-chain for free, a bulk exit costs about a bare spend, and a solo exit
costs about ten times one but is a single transaction with no timelock and
no counterparty. All figures in this and the following sections use the
model of \S\ref{sec:eval}. Figures marked \emph{estimate} are built from
measured sizes for scripts or populations not measured directly.

\paragraph{Exit.} Figure~\ref{fig:anatomy} decomposes exits. The FULL
exit at $D = 16$ is $1{,}020\vB$, or $921\vB$ with the fee taken from the
payout. The hypothetical native opcode's $388\vB$ is measured on the
six-leaf tree, whose compressed proof is $56\vB$ of witness; with $2^{16}$
members the proof grows by about 14 non-empty siblings to roughly
$530\vB$, which is the better estimate of the floor for a design that
publishes a proof in script. The transaction-tree model and its
sensitivity to radix and round size are in \S\ref{sec:eval} and
Table~\ref{tab:ark}; it ignores the leaf's timelock and the fee of each
of the exiter's transactions. In bulk the cost per leaf depends on who
signs. With \tr{EXIT}$_K$ every leaf carries its own path, so a second
exit adds $665\vB$; the operator's sweep of expired leases shares the
path and costs $203$ and $158\vB$ per leaf for 13 and 51 leaves at
$D = 16$ (and $147\vB$ for 206 leaves at $D = 20$), within 1.4 times a
bare spend, but only for members who accepted a lease. The owner-side
equivalent is the cohort rewrite of \S\ref{sec:batching}, at 359, 195 and
$158\vB$ per leaf for 4, 16 and 64 owners leaving together. The chain
limit counts transactions, not leaves.

\paragraph{Entry and moving value.} \tr{JOIN}$_h$ walks only the levels
above its slot, so at $D = 16$ an operator-funded 1{,}024-member graft
costs $614\vB$ (estimate), under $1\vB$ per member, against $43\vB$ for
one output in an on-chain batch and about $154\vB$ for a channel opened
in its own transaction (\S\ref{sec:graft}); a self-funded member adds
its own $58\vB$ input and the signing round of \S\ref{sec:parties}. An
on-chain \tr{UPDATE} is $1{,}030\vB$ and a voucher redemption
$1{,}614\vB$, so moving value by rewriting leaves would cost about ten times a bare
spend per payment. Value moves off-chain instead, as it does in
Lightning and Ark: in channel leaves as HTLC forwards
(\S\ref{sec:channels}), or by vouchers that the issuer usually settles
rather than the holder redeems (\S\ref{sec:receive}). \tr{UPDATE} is
maintenance, not payment.

\paragraph{Lifetime and latency.} Table~\ref{tab:lifetime} adds entry and
exit. The dense tree wins outright for a member who never exits alone or
who is swept, loses to Lightning and to a bare UTXO for a member who exits
alone, and beats an Ark unroll there by 1.3 to 2.5 times, depending on
the round's size; the sparse profile is the most expensive design in
every column. A LayerTree exit has no pre-signed transaction to lose, no
counterparty and no timelock, and the shared output does not expire,
though the member's lease does. It loses on the solo exit, the on-chain
per-leaf update, the sparse profile and serialisation through one
output.
