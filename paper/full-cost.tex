\section{Costs Against the Alternatives}\label{sec:cost}

Joining, updating and leaving a shared output each cost more bytes than
the same operation on a bare UTXO. What sharing buys is a different
placement of the cost: entry is nearly free in bulk, value moves
off-chain for free, a bulk exit costs about a bare spend, and a solo exit
costs about ten times one but is a single transaction with no timelock and
no counterparty. All figures in this and the following sections use the
model of \S\ref{sec:eval}. Figures marked \emph{estimate} are built from
measured sizes for scripts or populations not measured directly.

\paragraph{Exit.} Figure~\ref{fig:anatomy} decomposes exits; taking the
fee from the payout brings the FULL exit at $D = 16$ to $921\vB$. The
hypothetical native opcode's $388\vB$ is measured on the six-leaf tree,
whose compressed proof is $56\vB$ of witness; with $2^{16}$ members the
proof grows by about 14 non-empty siblings to roughly $530\vB$, the
better estimate of the floor for a design that publishes a proof in
script. The transaction-tree model (Table~\ref{tab:ark}) ignores the
leaf's timelock and the fee of each of the exiter's transactions. In
bulk the cost per leaf depends on who signs: with \tr{EXIT}$_K$ every
leaf carries its own path, so a second exit adds $665\vB$, while the
operator's sweep of expired leases (only for members who accepted a
lease) and the owners' cohort rewrite share the path and reach about
$160\vB$ per leaf, under 1.5 times a bare spend
(\S\ref{sec:batching}). The chain limit counts transactions, not
leaves.

\paragraph{Entry and moving value.} \tr{JOIN}$_h$ walks only the levels
above its slot, so at $D = 16$ an operator-funded 1{,}024-member graft
costs $614\vB$ (estimate, without a payout output), under $1\vB$ per member, against $43\vB$ for
one output in an on-chain batch and about $154\vB$ for a channel opened
in its own transaction (\S\ref{sec:graft}); a self-funded member adds
its own $58\vB$ input and the signing round of \S\ref{sec:parties}. An
on-chain \tr{UPDATE} is $1{,}030\vB$ and a voucher redemption
$1{,}617\vB$ (neither has a payout output; on the skeleton that carries
one, an \tr{UPDATE} is $1{,}073\vB$), so moving value by rewriting leaves would cost about ten times a bare
spend per payment. Value moves off-chain instead, as it does in
Lightning and Ark: in channel leaves as HTLC forwards
(\S\ref{sec:channels}), or by vouchers that the issuer usually settles
rather than the holder redeems (\S\ref{sec:receive}). \tr{UPDATE} is
maintenance, not payment.

\paragraph{Lifetime and latency.} Table~\ref{tab:lifetime} adds entry and
exit. The dense tree wins outright for a member who never exits alone or
who is swept, and loses to Lightning and to a bare UTXO for a member who
exits alone, though it beats an Ark unroll there; the sparse profile is
the most expensive design in every column. A LayerTree exit has no
pre-signed transaction to lose, no counterparty and no timelock, and the
shared output does not expire, though the member's lease does.
