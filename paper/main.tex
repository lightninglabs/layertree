\begin{abstract}
Constructions that let many users share one Bitcoin output pay at exit
time: a user who leaves publishes $O(\log N)$ transactions, the tree's
interior must exist as pre-signed or template-committed transactions, and
one unresponsive user forces the whole tree on-chain at expiry. We present
\textsc{LayerTree}, in which a single output commits to an off-chain
Merkle-sum tree of balances and tapscript covenants enforce every state
transition. Any member exits, and the operator evicts any expired member,
in \emph{one} transaction whose size grows with the depth of the tree
(about 32 virtual bytes per level) rather than with a path of
transactions: 1{,}020 virtual bytes at depth~16. Under a simple model of
an Ark- or CTV-style transaction tree of the same depth this is about 60\%
smaller; under mass exit, where the transaction tree's interior is shared,
the gap narrows. Membership changes need no interactive signing round,
the shared output never expires, and whole cohorts are added or swept in
one constant-shape transaction (158 to 203 virtual bytes per evicted leaf
for cohorts of 51 and 13).

The construction composes only opcodes already proposed for Bitcoin: the
restored string and arithmetic opcodes of tapleaf version \texttt{0xC2},
transaction introspection, signature checks over stack messages and
elliptic-curve tweaks. We realise every transition against the full
candidate set, all but one against a \texttt{0xC0}-only subset, and the
core exit against \op{CAT} plus one point-tweak opcode alone, by having
the script rebuild the BIP-341 signature message and check it with a
generator-key signature. Lightning channels are hosted inside leaves with prevout-independent
commitments, so a hub of $N$ channels occupies one output. We prove
conservation unconditionally, and proof soundness and payability under a
real-tree invariant, in the idealised hash model, mechanised in Lean~4. A
Python simulator, a byte-exact Go implementation and 338 executable
checks accompany the design.
\keywords{Bitcoin \and covenants \and Merkle-sum trees \and payment
channels \and layer two \and formal verification}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Payment channels~\cite{lightning} move Bitcoin activity off-chain for
pairs; the open problem is doing it for \emph{groups}: letting $N$ users
share one unspent transaction output (UTXO) without giving up the ability
of each to leave alone. Every deployed or proposed approach pays for the
sharing when it is dissolved. In channel factories~\cite{factories},
CoinPool~\cite{coinpool}, Ark~\cite{ark,clark}, timeout
trees~\cite{timeouttrees,superscalar} and covenant trees built on
\op{CHECKTEMPLATEVERIFY}~\cite{bip119} or
\op{CHECKCONTRACTVERIFY}~\cite{bip443,matt}, the shared output is the root
of a tree of \emph{transactions}. A member who leaves unilaterally must
publish the path from the root to its leaf, $O(\log N)$ transactions each
paying a fee; the interior of the tree must exist in advance as pre-signed
transactions (requiring every member to sign, and to be online) or as
covenant templates; and when the tree expires, reclaiming the funds of a
single unresponsive member means publishing everything.

This paper takes a different route. The tree stays off-chain and is a
tree of \emph{data}, not transactions: a binary Merkle-sum tree of
balances whose root is committed by one output. A member exits by
presenting its leaf and sibling path to the output itself; the spending
script recomputes the root from the proof, checks it against the
commitment, recomputes the root with the leaf removed, and requires the
transaction to recreate the output under the new root with the leaf's
amount paid out. The member needs nobody's signature, the exit is one
transaction, and its size grows with the tree's depth (about 32 virtual
bytes per level) rather than with a path of transactions. The same
covenant lets the operator evict expired members, graft a pre-built
subtree of new members in one step, and sweep an expired cohort cheaply.

This needs script capabilities that are proposed for Bitcoin but not yet
consensus: the restored string and arithmetic opcodes of a new tapleaf
version (BIPs~440 and 441~\cite{bip440,bip441}), transaction
introspection (\op{TX}~\cite{optx}), signature checks over stack data
(\op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}~\cite{bip348}) and elliptic-curve
arithmetic~\cite{ecops,bip449}; several already exist on
Liquid~\cite{elements}. We propose no opcode of our own, and part of the
contribution is a measured account of how little of the candidate set is
actually needed.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Construction} (\S\ref{sec:construction}). A shared output
  committing to a Merkle-sum tree through a data-tweaked taproot internal
  key, with transitions for unilateral exit, eviction, batched exit,
  subtree grafting, cooperative update, single-use vouchers and cohort
  sweeps, in a dense operator-assigned profile and a key-addressed sparse
  profile with compressed proofs.
\item \textbf{Realisation in three opcode tiers} (\S\ref{sec:scripts}).
  Every transition is implemented against the full candidate set; every
  transition but the cohort sweep against a \texttt{0xC0}-only subset; and
  the single-leaf exit, the load-bearing case, against \op{CAT} and one
  point-tweak opcode alone. The last tier replaces introspection by a
  constructive trick, rebuilding the BIP-341 signature message in script
  and checking a signature under the generator point. This gives an
  \emph{upper bound} on the opcodes the covenant needs; we prove no lower
  bound.
\item \textbf{Channel leaves} (\S\ref{sec:channels}). Lightning channels
  hosted in leaves, with prevout-independent commitments that emulate
  \texttt{ANYPREVOUT} using introspection and stack signature checks, and
  an update/settlement state machine, so a funding output need not exist
  until a dispute; and the three tree-layer rules shared-key leaves
  require.
\item \textbf{Security analysis with mechanised proofs}
  (\S\ref{sec:security}). Conservation, proof soundness and payability in
  the idealised hash model, checked in Lean~4. The proofs isolated a fact
  the informal argument had blurred: sums are conserved unconditionally,
  but exits are guaranteed only under a real-tree invariant that creation
  and grafting must establish off-chain.
\item \textbf{Implementation and measurement} (\S\ref{sec:eval}). A
  tapscript simulator for the candidate opcodes, a byte-exact Go
  implementation, and measured costs: a $1{,}020\vB$ exit at depth~16, a
  $665\vB$ marginal batched exit, cohort eviction at 158 to $203\vB$ per
  leaf, and fees from 1 to 250 sat/vB.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

A pay-to-taproot output~\cite{bip341} commits to an internal key $P$ and
the Merkle root $m$ of a \emph{taptree} of scripts through
$Q = P + H_{\mathsf{TapTweak}}(P \,\|\, m)\cdot G$, and is spent either by
a Schnorr signature~\cite{bip340} under $Q$ or by revealing one script
with its Merkle path (the \emph{control block}) and satisfying it.
Tapscript~\cite{bip342} has no loops, so every ``loop'' below is unrolled
and costs bytes per iteration. BIP-441~\cite{bip441} defines tapleaf
version \texttt{0xC2}, restoring the string opcodes disabled in 2010
(\op{CAT}, \op{SUBSTR}, \op{LEFT}, \op{RIGHT}, bit operations) with
arithmetic on unsigned numbers of any length, metered by
BIP-440~\cite{bip440} (``varops'', $10{,}000$ units per weight unit of the
transaction); BIP-347~\cite{bip347} proposes \op{CAT} alone in the current
version. \op{TX}~\cite{optx} pushes selected fields of the spending
transaction, optionally collated into one hash preimage;
\op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}~\cite{bip348} verifies a Schnorr signature over a
stack element; \op{TWEAKADD}~\cite{bip449} computes $x(P + t G)$, and the
EC-ops draft~\cite{ecops} adds point addition, scalar multiplication and
negation. All are proposals; we note where the design depends on open
details. A Merkle-sum tree~\cite{merkle,mssmt} hashes
each node's children together with their sums, so an inclusion proof
proves a leaf's contribution to the root sum; sparse Merkle
trees~\cite{smt} address positions by hash, making non-membership an
inclusion proof of an empty leaf.

\section{Model and Goals}\label{sec:model}

A \emph{tree} has one \emph{operator} and any number of \emph{members}.
Each member owns a \emph{leaf}: a balance and three public keys (an owner
key that authorises the member's actions, an exit key paid on exit, and
an evictor key, normally the operator's, that may remove the leaf after a
lease height). The operator holds no key over the funds, only an
\emph{operator key} that authorises additions and co-signs cooperative
changes. The \emph{tree output} is the on-chain UTXO; its amount equals
the sum committed by the root and its script commits to the root.

We assume the Bitcoin consensus rules with the opcodes of
\S\ref{sec:background} active, collision-resistant tagged hashing
(SHA-256), the discrete-logarithm assumption on secp256k1, and
unforgeable Schnorr signatures. The adversary controls the operator, any
subset of members, and the order of transactions within the rules; it
may refuse to serve data. We want:

\begin{description}
\item[G1 Conservation.] No transition creates value: the output's amount
  equals the sum committed by its root, and payouts are funded by removed
  leaf sums. Under the real-tree invariant of \S\ref{sec:security} that
  sum is the members' balances.
\item[G2 Unilateral single-transaction exit.] A member holding its leaf
  and path can construct an accepted exit paying its balance in one
  transaction of $O(D)$ bytes with no other party's signature, given a fee
  source, a balance above dust, and the contention bound of
  \S\ref{sec:security}.
\item[G3 Eviction.] After a leaf's lease height its evictor can remove it,
  paying the balance to the exit key.
\item[G4 Non-interactive membership.] Adding or removing members needs no
  signing round among members and no pre-signed transactions.
\item[G5 No expiry.] The shared output has no lifetime; leases are per
  leaf and renewable.
\end{description}
G1 and G2 are the subject of the theorems of \S\ref{sec:security}; G3 to
G5 hold by construction (eviction is an authorisation branch of the exit
script; no transition needs a signature but the actor's; no script carries
an expiry) and are outside the verified perimeter. Liveness of joins is
not a goal, and data availability is a member's responsibility.

\section{Construction}\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{The Tree}\label{sec:tree}

We write $\mathsf{le}_{k}(x)$ for the $k$-byte little-endian encoding of
$x$. Fix a depth $D$. A tree has $2^D$ positions, each empty or holding a
leaf. A leaf is a fixed 116-byte record:
\begin{multline*}
\lesf(\mathsf{amount}) \,\|\, \mathsf{exit\_key} \,\|\, \mathsf{owner\_key}
\,\|\, \mathsf{evictor\_key} \\
\,\|\, \mathsf{le}_{32}(\mathsf{evict\_after})
\,\|\, \mathsf{le}_{32}(\mathsf{nonce}) \,\|\, \mathsf{le}_{32}(\mathsf{seq}),
\end{multline*}
with keys as 32-byte x-only points, $\mathsf{evict\_after}$ a block
height, $\mathsf{nonce}$ a per-leaf identifier and $\mathsf{seq}$ a
counter advanced by vouchers (Appendix~\ref{app:claim}). A \emph{node} is
a pair $(h, s)$ of a 32-byte hash and an unsigned sum. A position holding
leaf $\ell$ has node $(\Hleaf(\ell), \ell.\mathsf{amount})$; an empty
position has node $\Emp_0 = (0^{32}, 0)$. The parent of $(h_L, s_L)$ and
$(h_R, s_R)$ is
\[
\bigl(\Hnode(h_L \,\|\, \encs(s_L) \,\|\, h_R \,\|\, \encs(s_R)),\; s_L + s_R\bigr),
\qquad \encs(s) = \lesf(s) \,\|\, \mathtt{0x01}.
\]
Every hash in this paper is a BIP-340 tagged hash with its own tag, so
no preimage of one is a preimage of another. $\encs(s)$ is chosen so that
a script produces it from a computed sum with one addition: the minimal
BIP-441 encoding of $s + 2^{64}$ is $\lesf(s)\,\|\,\mathtt{01}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:enc}). We write $\Emp_h$ for the node of the empty tree of
height $h$; these constants are hard-coded in scripts, and $\Emp_D$ is
the empty tree's root.

The \emph{sibling path} $\pi$ of a position is the list of $D$ pairs
$(\mathsf{dir}_k, (h_k, s_k))$, one per level from the root down, giving
the direction the position lies on ($\mathsf{R}$ if the node being proved
is the right child) and the sibling node not taken. $\fold(n, \pi)$
combines a node $n$ with a path level by level from the bottom up: at
each level it hashes $n$ with the sibling in the order the direction
gives, with the two sums, and adds the sums. The witness of
\S\ref{sec:scripts} supplies the same pairs deepest level first, because
the script consumes them in that order. The two facts that drive the
design are that the honest path folds a leaf's node to the root, and that
folding a \emph{different} node up the \emph{same} path yields the root
of the tree with that position replaced (\S\ref{sec:security}).

\paragraph{Profiles.}
In the \emph{dense} profile positions are assigned by the operator and a
path carries explicit direction bits; each level costs 88 bytes of script
and 41 bytes of witness. In the \emph{sparse} profile a leaf's position is
the low $D$ bits of $\Hpos(\mathsf{owner\_key} \,\|\, \mathsf{nonce})$,
direction bits are derived in script from the position hash, and empty
siblings are elided from the witness (a one-byte marker in place of a
node; the script substitutes $\Emp_k$), so witness data grows with
$\log_2 N$ for $N$ members rather than with $D$. The sparse profile is
the Merkle-sum sparse Merkle tree of Taproot Assets~\cite{mssmt}
truncated to $D = 32$ or $64$: key-addressed positions, a
history-independent root and structural uniqueness of leaves, at 1.8 to
2.7 times the per-exit cost of the dense profile at comparable membership
(Table~\ref{tab:sizes}).

\subsection{The Tree Output}\label{sec:output}

Let $r$ be the root hash and $S$ the root sum. The tree output is a
taproot output with internal key
\[
P = \mathsf{lift\_x}\bigl(x(\NUMS + \Hstate(r)\cdot G)\bigr),
\]
where $\NUMS$ is the BIP-341 nothing-up-my-sleeve point and
$\mathsf{lift\_x}$ the BIP-340 even-$y$ normalisation, so the key path is
unspendable and every spend goes through a script. Its taptree contains
one leaf per supported transition script; the taptree's Merkle root $m$
identifies the rule set and is constant for the tree's life. We write
$\TreeSpk(r, m)$ for the resulting scriptPubKey. A tree output for state
$(r, S)$ is an output with script $\TreeSpk(r, m)$ and amount $S$.

Every transition script receives $r$ and $m$ in its witness, recomputes
$\TreeSpk(r, m)$ and checks it against the spent input's scriptPubKey,
read with \op{TX}. This binds both the root the proofs are checked
against and the script set the successor carries; the scripts cannot
hard-code $m$ since $m$ depends on them, and binding it is security
critical, or a spender could recreate the tree under scripts of its
choosing. Since every script checks input~0, two trees cannot be merged or
spent atomically.

\subsection{Transitions}\label{sec:transitions}

Every transition spends the tree output as input~0 and recreates it as
output~0 with the new root and the new total; payouts, if any, occupy
outputs $1, \dots, K$; all other inputs and outputs are unconstrained, so
fees, change and anchors are free. The common core of every transition is
the \emph{step}: given a path $\pi$, an old node and a new node, check
that the old node folds to the committed root $(r, S)$, with $S$ read as
input~0's amount (\tr{CHECK\_OLD}), and compute the root the new node
folds to; a transition is a sequence of steps, each proving its old node
against the root the previous one produced (\tr{CHECK\_PREV}), followed
by a check that output~0 is $(S', \TreeSpk(r', m))$ unless $r' = \Emp_D$
(\tr{FINAL}). Appendix~\ref{app:scripts} gives the pseudocode and the
88-byte tapscript of one level; Appendix~\ref{app:listings} lists every
script of every tier in full.



\paragraph{\tr{EXIT}.} Removes one leaf $\ell$: the old node is
$(\Hleaf(\ell), \ell.\mathsf{amount})$, the new node $\Emp_0$, and
output~1 must pay $\mathrm{P2TR}(\ell.\mathsf{exit\_key})$. A witness
flag selects the authorisation. An \emph{owner exit} needs a signature
by $\ell.\mathsf{owner\_key}$ over the transaction and, in the FULL
tier, permits output~1 to be at most $\ell.\mathsf{amount}$ (the owner
consents to the slack as fee by signing). An \emph{eviction} needs a
signature by $\ell.\mathsf{evictor\_key}$, a lock time of at least
$\ell.\mathsf{evict\_after}$, and output~1 \emph{equal} to
$\ell.\mathsf{amount}$: an evictor may remove but never shortchange.
Signatures must cover all outputs, or a relayer could redirect the slack.
The exact-payout variant \tr{EXIT}$^{=}$ requires equality for owner
exits too; it is a tree-creation parameter, the only form in MID and MIN,
and matters for shared-key leaves (\S\ref{sec:channels}).

\paragraph{\tr{JOIN}$_h$.} Grafts a subtree of height $h$ into an empty
aligned slot: the old node is the constant $\Emp_h$, the new node
$(h_{\mathrm{new}}, s_{\mathrm{new}})$ is supplied and signed by the
operator, and output~0 rises by $s_{\mathrm{new}}$, funded by other
inputs. With $h = 8$ this adds up to 256 members for a constant script
cost. The covenant cannot verify the grafted interior; prospective members
verify their own paths inside it before funding (\S\ref{sec:security}).
\tr{JOINK}$_K$ sequences $K$ single-leaf joins under one operator
signature, the batching primitive of the sparse profile, where grafting
is unavailable.

\paragraph{\tr{UPDATE}.} Replaces one leaf by another at the same position
with signatures from both the owner and the operator; the output amount
moves by the difference. This is the splice, the lease renewal and the
ownership transfer. A variant \tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$ additionally requires a
signature by the \emph{old} leaf's exit key, for leaves whose exit key is
shared with a third party (\S\ref{sec:channels}).

\paragraph{\tr{CLAIM}.} Executes a \emph{voucher}, an off-chain
owner-signed instruction to carve a new leaf out of an existing one,
verified with \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}; the total is unchanged and replay
is impossible because the first of its two steps changes the source leaf.
Vouchers are optional and orthogonal to the rest of the design;
Appendix~\ref{app:claim} specifies them.

\paragraph{Composition.} \tr{EXIT}$_K$ applies $K$ exits in one
transaction, each with its own authorisation, paying outputs $1..K$; the
$k$-th step proves its leaf against the root step $k-1$ produced. The
exiters must agree on the transaction, but nothing is pre-signed and no
one outside the batch is involved. \tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$ is a
\emph{shape-specialised multiproof}: one sibling path to an aligned
subtree of height $h$ and the $2^h$ leaves below it (or empty markers);
the script recomputes the subtree root in post-order, checks each leaf's
evictor key and expired lease, pays each leaf to its exit key at the next
unused output, and replaces the subtree by $\Emp_h$, hashing the interior
once rather than once per leaf. A flag-driven generic multiproof was
measured and rejected: without loops it saves no script. If the new
root is $\Emp_D$ the tree is empty and output~0 is unconstrained, so a
tree dissolves without leaving a zero-value output.

same in every tier. Coverage is not uniform: FULL implements every
transition in both profiles, MID everything but the cohort sweep, and MIN
the dense single-leaf exit only, the other transitions following the same
construction but being argued, not measured.

\subsection{The FULL Tier}
With \texttt{0xC2} the leaf is one 116-byte stack element read with
\op{SUBSTR}; sums use arbitrary-length arithmetic and are padded with
$\langle 2^{64}\rangle\ \op{ADD}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:enc}); one \tr{LEVEL}
of the fold is 88 bytes (Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}). $\TreeSpk$ is
recomputed with the EC opcodes ($\Hstate(r)\cdot G + \NUMS$, tweaked by
the taptree hash, wrapped in a P2TR script). The \tr{EXIT}$_1$ prologue,
with both introspections and the key recomputation, is under 700 bytes.

\subsection{The MID Tier}
Tapleaf \texttt{0xC0} keeps BIP-342 semantics: no splice opcodes and
4-byte signed numbers. Fields arrive as separate witness elements, each
checked with \op{SIZE} and re-joined with \op{CAT} before hashing, and a
computed sum is widened to 8 bytes by a witness-supplied zero pad checked
with \op{SIZE} and \op{CAT} (\textsf{PADZ}, Appendix~\ref{app:scripts});
\texttt{MINIMALDATA} policy means lock times and positions arrive as
minimal numbers plus a pad. A cap of $2^{31}$ satoshis per tree and exact
payouts are the price of 4-byte arithmetic. The tier assumes \op{TX} in
\texttt{0xC0}, which the draft leaves open; Liquid~\cite{elements} has
per-field introspection in the same role today.

\subsection{The MIN Tier: Introspection from a Signature}\label{sec:min}
The covenant must read the input's scriptPubKey and amount and
outputs~0 and~1. Without \op{TX} the only opcode that sees the transaction
is \op{CHECKSIG}, and we turn it into introspection. The script
\emph{constructs} the BIP-341 signature message from values it has
derived (the recomputed $\TreeSpk(r,m)$, the input amount, the new tree
output, the payout) and raw witness pieces for everything it does not
constrain (the fee input, prevouts, sequences, version, lock time, tapleaf
hash), hashes it, and demands a Schnorr signature under the generator $G$
with nonce $R = G$. For public key $G$ and nonce $G$ the challenge is
$e = H_{\mathsf{challenge}}(G_x \,\|\, G_x \,\|\, \mathsf{msg})$ and the
verification equation $s\cdot G = R + e\cdot P = (1 + e)\cdot G$ has the
unique solution $s = e + 1 \bmod n$; $G$ has even $y$, so
$\mathsf{lift\_x}(G_x) = G$ on both sides and the even-$y$ check on $R$
passes. The script computes $e$ from its constructed message, forms $s$,
and calls \op{CHECKSIG} with the signature $G_x \,\|\, s$ and public key
$G_x$. Since \op{CHECKSIG} hashes the real transaction, the check passes
only if the constructed message equals the real one, up to a collision in
the tagged hashes; every field the script fixed is thereby enforced.

Three caveats. The ``signature'' authorises nothing (anyone can produce
it; authorisation is the separate owner or evictor \op{CHECKSIGVERIFY});
what it adds is binding, resting on collision resistance of the TapSighash
and challenge hashes, an assumption outside the Lean model of
\S\ref{sec:security}. Without arbitrary-precision arithmetic, $e + 1$ is
formed by grinding: the spender varies the fee input's sequence number
(within the range that neither imposes a BIP-68 lock nor disables BIP-125
signalling) until the last byte of $e$ is $\mathtt{00}$ and supplies the
first 31 bytes; the script checks $e_{[0,31)} \,\|\, \mathtt{00} = e$ and
uses $e_{[0,31)} \,\|\, \mathtt{01}$ as $s$. This takes 256 trials on
average, must precede signing the fee input, and makes fee bumping a
matter of a child spending a free output rather than replacement. In the
$2^{-128}$ event that $e \ge n - 1$ the signature does not parse and the
spender re-grinds. The script guarantees that input~0 is a tree output
with amount $S$ and script $\TreeSpk(r, m)$, that there are exactly two
inputs, that input~0 carries no annex, and that outputs~0 and~1 are as the
covenant requires; everything else is free but must be presented
consistently (Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}).

The transaction shape is fixed per script, the tree output key is
recomputed with two \op{TWEAKADD}s, and \tr{EXIT}$_1$ costs $3{,}572$
bytes of script at $D = 16$ (70\% more than FULL) for a transaction of
$1{,}456\vB$ (43\% more). Vouchers are impossible without a signature over
stack data, and so are channel leaves. The result is an upper bound:
BIP-342 tapscript, \op{CAT} and one point-tweak opcode realise the exit
covenant. Every restored splice and bit opcode, \op{TX} and the EC point
opcodes are conveniences: for the dense exit at $D = 16$, dropping the
splice and EC opcodes costs 260 witness units and dropping \op{TX} as well
1{,}744. Whether the largest scripts (a 64-leaf sweep is 16 kB in FULL)
fit MIN's constraints is open; we claim no lower bound.

\section{Channel Leaves}\label{sec:channels}

A leaf whose exit key is a 2-of-2 between its owner and a counterparty
(normally the operator) can host a payment channel that exists only
off-chain and whose funding output materialises at the payout of the
transaction that removes the leaf: a tree of $N$ such leaves is a
Lightning hub behind one UTXO, with opening, resizing and closing costing
nothing on-chain except at dispute. Appendix~\ref{app:channels} gives
scripts, state machine and costs.

\paragraph{Rebindable commitments.}
The funding outpoint is unknown when channel states are signed, and it
changes with every tree transition. Commitment transactions must
therefore be authorised by signatures that do not commit to the prevout,
which \op{TX} and \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK} provide as an emulation of
\texttt{SIGHASH\_ANYPREVOUT}~\cite{bip118}: the script collates, with one
\op{TX} call, the fields the signature covers (version, lock time, the
current input's sequence, the number of inputs, and all outputs),
prefixes a per-channel context constant baked into the script, hashes,
and verifies a BIP-340 signature under the channel key with
\op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}. The context commits to the tree identifier $m$,
the leaf identity, the channel key, the capacity and a splice epoch, so a
state signed for one channel or capacity never verifies for another;
committing the number of inputs (one) prevents two funding outputs with
the same script from being spent by one commitment. The input amount is
not committed by the update signature: the committed outputs sum to the
capacity, so an input holding more would only burn the excess to fees,
and rule (i) below makes it hold exactly the capacity.

\paragraph{State machine.}
Penalty channels cannot be used, since a penalty transaction must be
pre-signed against a specific outpoint. Channel leaves use the
update/settlement ratchet of eltoo~\cite{eltoo,lnsymmetry}: the update
transaction for state $n$ carries lock time $S_0 + n$ ($S_0$ the start of
the timestamp range) and spends the funding output or any earlier update
output through a leaf admitting only later lock times; the settlement
spends state $n$'s update output after a relative delay
$\mathsf{csv\_delay}$ through a leaf whose signature also commits to the
spent script, so it binds only to its own state. Both are zero-fee
version-3 transactions with an ephemeral anchor~\cite{bip431}. A responder
needs the channel context and the counterparty's latest signature but no
per-state script, since the update output's taptree is a function of the
context and $n$, which is published in the lock time.

\paragraph{HTLCs and deadlines.}
Settlement outputs include one per in-flight HTLC, with no second stage
since the settlement is itself delayed; payments between channel leaves
of one tree are HTLC forwards through the operator and never touch the
tree output. The deadline budget an HTLC needs is
\[
\mathsf{cltv\_expiry\_delta} \;\ge\; G_{\mathrm{exit}} + 1 + 1 + \mathsf{csv\_delay} + 1 + G_{\mathrm{sweep}},
\]
where $G_{\mathrm{exit}}$ is the time to get the exit confirmed
\emph{including contention for the tree output}, then one block each for
the update and settlement transactions, the delay between them, and the
HTLC sweep. With $\mathsf{csv\_delay} = 144$ and a conservative
$G_{\mathrm{exit}} = 72$ this is about 220 blocks, against 40 to 80 on
the public network; and $G_{\mathrm{exit}}$ grows with the number of
members under the contention bound of \S\ref{sec:security}, so the budget
is a function of tree size.

\paragraph{Three tree-layer rules.}
Three tree rules were too lenient for a leaf whose balance is not the
owner's alone. (i) The owner's fee-from-payout option would let the owner
underfund the channel and hold the counterparty's balance hostage, so
channel leaves require exact payouts and their trees should use
\tr{EXIT}$^{=}$, visible in $m$. (ii) A voucher lets the owner carve
value out of a leaf alone, so channel leaves set $\mathsf{seq}$ to the
value whose successor does not fit in four bytes and thereby cannot be
claimed: $\mathtt{0xFFFFFFFF}$ under BIP-441 numerics but
$\mathtt{0x7FFFFFFF}$ under \texttt{CScriptNum}, where
$\mathtt{ff\,ff\,ff\,ff}$ encodes $-(2^{31}-1)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:enc}).
(iii) Before the lease height the counterparty can enforce nothing
on-chain, so a routing channel leaf sets it to zero. For channels between
two members, \tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$ prevents the owner and operator from
splicing the leaf down behind the counterparty's back. Channel leaves need
\op{TX} and \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}, hence FULL or MID; MIN's constructive
introspection necessarily commits to the prevout.

\section{Security Analysis}\label{sec:security}

We work in the idealised hash model: $\Hleaf$ and $\Hnode$ are injective
on the domains the protocol uses, and digests under different tags never
coincide or equal the zero string; a proof under injectivity holds against
any adversary that cannot exhibit a collision. The theorems are stated for
the abstract rules of \S\ref{sec:transitions} and mechanised in
Lean~4~\cite{lean4} in about 1{,}300 lines without Mathlib
(Appendix~\ref{app:lean}); bracketed names are Lean identifiers. The
correspondence between rules and bytecode is by execution
(\S\ref{sec:eval}), not proof.

\begin{lemma}[Encodings]\label{lem:enc}
For $s < 2^{64}$, the minimal BIP-441 encoding of $s + 2^{64}$ is
$\lesf(s)\,\|\,\mathtt{01}$; leaf and node serialisations are injective
under the field bounds; the minimal encoding of $2^{32}$ has five bytes
while that of $2^{32}-1$ has four; and the \texttt{CScriptNum} of
$-(2^{31}-1)$ is $\mathtt{ff\,ff\,ff\,ff}$ with successor
$\mathtt{fe\,ff\,ff\,ff}$.
[\lean{enc64\_eq\_minimal},\allowbreak\ \lean{serializeLeaf\_inj},\allowbreak\
\lean{serializeNode\_inj},\allowbreak\ \lean{minimalLE\_two\_pow\_32\_length},\allowbreak\
\lean{scriptNum\_neg\_max31\_succ}]
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Binding]\label{thm:binding}
Roots bind trees: two trees of the same depth with equal roots are equal.
Any node $n$ and path $\pi$ with $\fold(n, \pi)$ equal to the root of a
tree $T$ are the root of one of $T$'s subtrees and that subtree's honest
path. In particular the sum in $n$ is the true sum of that subtree, and a
leaf node that verifies with a full-length path is the leaf at that
position. [\lean{root\_inj},\allowbreak\ \lean{fold\_sound}]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[sketch]
Induction on the path. A non-empty path claims an internal node, so the
tree is not a tip by tag separation. Injectivity of $\Hnode$ on the
parent's preimage equates both children's hashes \emph{and} sums with the
claimed ones, and the induction hypothesis applies to the child the
direction bit selects. This is the only place the hash axioms are used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conservation]\label{thm:cons}
For any sequence of valid steps from root $(r, S)$ to $(r', S')$,
$S' + \sum s_{\mathrm{old}} = S + \sum s_{\mathrm{new}}$. Hence for
\tr{EXIT}$_K$, $S' + \sum_k \mathrm{payout}_k \le S$, with equality under
exact payouts; for \tr{JOIN}$_h$, $S' = S + s_{\mathrm{new}}$; for
\tr{UPDATE}, $S' + a_{\mathrm{old}} = S + a_{\mathrm{new}}$; and
\tr{CLAIM} leaves $S$ unchanged.
[\lean{Steps.sum},\allowbreak\ \lean{ExitK.no\_inflation},\allowbreak\
\lean{ExitK.exact\_conservation},\allowbreak\ \lean{join\_sound},\allowbreak\
\lean{update\_sound},\allowbreak\ \lean{claim\_sound}]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[sketch]
A fold adds exactly the sibling sums it is shown, so the two folds of a
step differ by $s_{\mathrm{new}} - s_{\mathrm{old}}$; the rest is
induction on the sequence. No hash assumption is needed: the covenant
conserves sums whatever nodes it is shown.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Payability]\label{thm:pay}
For any tree $T$ and position $p$ holding leaf $\ell$, the honest witness
(the leaf, its path, $\Emp_0$) is a valid step from $T$'s root to the
root of $T$ with $p$ emptied, the sum falls by $\ell.\mathsf{amount}$,
and every other position is unchanged. [\lean{payable}]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Real-tree invariant]\label{thm:ground}
If the current root is the root of a tree and every step is well formed
and \emph{grounded} (its new node is the root of some tree of the step's
height), the final root is the root of a tree of the same depth.
Leaf-level steps are grounded by construction. [\lean{Steps.sound}]
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Establishing and maintaining the invariant.} Theorems
\ref{thm:binding} and \ref{thm:pay} are stated for a tree, while the
on-chain object is a root. The invariant that the committed root is the
root of a real tree whose total is the output amount has a base case,
creation, which the covenant cannot enforce: a founding member must
verify its path against $r_0$ and the funding amount before contributing
(a wrong amount yields an output no script can spend, never a theft). Its
step is Theorem~\ref{thm:ground}: every transition but a \tr{JOIN} graft
is grounded by construction, and a graft is grounded exactly when the
operator's $(h_{\mathrm{new}}, s_{\mathrm{new}})$ has a preimage tree. If
it does not, Theorem~\ref{thm:cons} still holds, so no value is created,
but nothing below the node can ever be proven: the grafted value is
frozen. The covenant protects the \emph{sum}; members protect their
\emph{membership} by verifying their paths inside a subtree before funding
a join, which the specification makes mandatory. Under the invariant,
Theorems~\ref{thm:cons} and~\ref{thm:pay} give G1 and G2: whoever holds a
leaf and its path can construct an accepted exit paying the leaf amount
without anyone's cooperation or touching anyone else's leaf. The
mechanisation also showed that \tr{CLAIM} requires the claim position to
be empty \emph{after} the source step, so a drained source may serve as
the claim position; this is harmless.

\paragraph{Root binding to the output.} The scripts conclude that the
witness root and taptree are the committed ones from
$\TreeSpk(r', m') = \TreeSpk(r, m)$, which needs $\TreeSpk$ injective in
$(r, m)$ [\lean{witness\_root\_binds}]; Appendix~\ref{app:lean} reduces
this to the discrete logarithm of $\NUMS$ and collisions in $\Hstate$ or
the taproot tweak hash. Without binding $m$ a spender could recreate the
tree under scripts of its own.

\paragraph{Authorisation and lock times.} These decide who may perform a
transition, not what it does to the tree, and the theorems carry them as
opaque propositions; G3 to G5 rest on them. The requirement that exit
signatures cover all outputs matters because the FULL covenant only
bounds output~1's amount for owner exits; with \texttt{SIGHASH\_NONE} a
relayer could divert the slack.

\paragraph{Concurrency and pinning.} All transitions spend one UTXO, so
unconfirmed transitions form a descendant chain of the last confirmed
tree output. Under today's default policy a package holds at most 25
transactions~\cite{bip125}, about a block's worth of transitions per
block; cluster mempool~\cite{clustermempool} changes the bound, not the
serialisation. The bound applies to joins and sweeps as well as exits, and
the chain invites pinning: a low-feerate exit early in it caps the feerate
of everything behind it, and replacing it means paying for its
descendants. Mitigations are cooperative batching, anchors so either party
can bump, and sizing trees by exit throughput rather than depth: 10{,}000
channel leaves need about 400 blocks to force-close while HTLC deadlines
run. This is the design's honest weak spot.

\paragraph{Data availability and reorganisations.} Members retain their
leaf and path and process every spend of the tree output; since each
witness contains the full sibling path it used, a member updates its path
from the chain alone and never depends on the operator to exit. A
reorganisation that drops a transition invalidates paths derived from it,
so members treat transitions as final only at their confirmation depth.

\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}

\paragraph{Implementation.} A Python tapscript interpreter for both leaf
versions models BIP-441 numerics, \op{TX}, \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}, the
EC opcodes and varops accounting, computes the real BIP-341 signature
message and enforces \texttt{MINIMALDATA}. Every implemented script runs
against a reference tree with positive and negative cases per rule
(forged leaf, stale root, attacker-chosen $m$, unconserved amount,
underfunded join, early eviction, evictor shortfall, unsigned update,
voucher replay, over-claim, stale second path in a batch): 272 FULL, 59
MID and 7 MIN checks, failing if any script uses an opcode outside the
candidate set. A Go implementation on \texttt{btcd} and \texttt{lnd}
generates the same scripts, byte-exact against 30 golden vectors, and its
BIP-341 signature hash equals the simulator's, which makes the MIN tier
testable end to end.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

CoinPool~\cite{coinpool} is the closest prior design: a shared UTXO with
per-user balances and unilateral withdrawal, realised with pre-signed or
covenant-committed transactions, so exit and update remain transaction
trees. Channel factories~\cite{factories} need all $N$ members to sign
every change and publish a tree to exit. Ark~\cite{ark} batches users
into rounds whose virtual outputs are leaves of a pre-signed tree;
covenant Ark~\cite{clark} removes the signing round with CTV or CCV
templates but keeps the tree, its unrolled exit and its expiry. Timeout
trees~\cite{timeouttrees} and SuperScalar~\cite{superscalar} combine
covenant trees with Decker-Wattenhofer factories and laddered expiries;
MATT-style trees~\cite{matt,bip443} replace signatures by templates.
Statechains~\cite{statechains} transfer a whole UTXO off-chain but do not
divide it; \op{VAULT}~\cite{bip345} is an introspection covenant that,
like ours, recreates its output under a modified commitment. LayerTree
differs from all of these in that the tree is data rather than
transactions: exit is one transaction of $O(D)$ bytes, the interior needs
no pre-signing, the operator can evict, and the output never expires
(Table~\ref{tab:compare}, Appendix~\ref{app:eval}).

The Merkle-sum sparse Merkle tree and its compressed proofs come from
Taproot Assets~\cite{mssmt}; here the tree is truncated and verified in
script. The data-tweaked internal key follows
\op{CHECKCONTRACTVERIFY}~\cite{bip443}. The channel state machine is
eltoo~\cite{eltoo}, with \texttt{ANYPREVOUT}~\cite{bip118} emulated by
collated introspection and \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK}. Liquid's
tapscript~\cite{elements} has \op{CAT}, \op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK},
per-field introspection, 64-bit arithmetic and point-tweak verification,
so the MID tier is close to expressible there today;
Simplicity~\cite{simplicity} would express the covenant directly. We know
of no prior use of a generator-key signature with $s = e + 1$ to make a
script's sighash reconstruction binding (\S\ref{sec:min}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

The covenant guarantees conservation and the payability of every leaf
under a grounded node. It does not guarantee that the operator will add
you (a member who is never added has lost nothing), that grafted nodes
have preimages, or that the tree was created correctly; the specification
makes the members' checks mandatory and the Lean development shows they
are load-bearing. The operator cannot steal, freeze provable leaves or
block exits, but it can evict any leaf after its lease and, in a cohort
tree, many at once for about $160\vB$ each, which makes griefing cheap;
members who want a hard guarantee buy it with a future lease height and,
for channel leaves, give up routing until it passes. Everything funnels
through one UTXO, so an uncooperative mass exit is bounded to roughly a
block's worth of transitions per block and exposed to pinning
(\S\ref{sec:security}); trees should be sized by this throughput and
sharded, and this cannot be engineered away within the single-UTXO
design. The design is hostage to proposals: a tapleaf version with
restored string opcodes or at least \op{CAT}, \op{TX} in \texttt{0xC0}
for the MID tier, and the EC opcodes' drafts; every selector and opcode
number is a named constant in the implementation, and a Liquid prototype
of the MID tier is the obvious next experiment. Future work: a P model~\cite{plang} of the off-chain protocol under an
adversarial scheduler, a verified interpreter connecting the Lean rules to
the bytecode, and completing the MIN tier.

\section{Conclusion}

A shared output whose tree lives off-chain as data rather than
transactions gives every member a one-transaction exit of about a
kilobyte, gives the operator eviction and cohort sweeps, needs no
pre-signing and never expires, using only opcodes already on the table.
Its guarantees are mechanically checked theorems, its costs are measured,
and its price is that all activity funnels through one UTXO, which bounds
how fast a tree can be dissolved against its will.
