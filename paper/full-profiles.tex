\section{Node Format and Radix}\label{sec:profiles}

The tree is a Merkle-sum tree whose position assignment, in the dense
profile, is the operator's. This section gives the node format and the
choice of radix; the sparse alternative is in
Appendix~\ref{app:sparse}.

\paragraph{One node format.} A parent hashes each child's sum, not only
their total, and this is a soundness property. A tree hashing $h_L \,\|\,
h_R \,\|\, (s_L + s_R)$ admits inflation: two leaves of 100 under a root of
sum 100 each have a proof whose sibling claims sum zero, and together the
proofs claim 200. Here the two proofs hash the distinct preimages $(A,
100, B, 0)$ and $(A, 0, B, 100)$, so at most one reaches the root; in Lean
this is \lean{serializeNode\_inj} with \lean{hNode\_inj}, from which
\lean{fold\_sound} gives that a verified leaf carries its true amount.

\paragraph{Dense positions.} An exit proves inclusion and a join proves
that its slot is $\Emp_h$, the same way in either profile. What the dense
profile adds is aligned subtrees, on which \tr{JOIN}$_h$,
\tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$ and every shape of \S\ref{sec:batching} depend:
cohort grafts at one price and sweeps at about $160\vB$ per leaf, at the
price of an operator who keeps the slot map (spec~5.4,
\path{spec/layertree.md}).

\paragraph{Radix.} A radix-$r$ path carries $41(r-1)D/\log_2 r$ sibling
bytes, $41D$ at radix 2 and $61.5D$ at radix 4, so radix 2 minimises the
witness and a higher radix can only save the fixed part of the unrolled
level. Table~\ref{tab:radix} measures it. Of the radix-4 level's 146 bytes about 62
are hashing and sums shared with radix 2 and the rest sibling and
direction handling, so the fixed part a higher radix amortises is about
60 bytes while the sibling part is what it multiplies; radix 4 comes out
about 2\% larger overall. With a native Merkle-sum opcode a
level costs only its sibling and radix 2 is optimal. The sparse profile's
radix is in Appendix~\ref{app:sparse}.
