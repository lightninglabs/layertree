\section{Profiles and Radix}\label{sec:profiles}

Both profiles are the same Merkle-sum tree. They differ in who decides
where a leaf sits, and that choice sets the depth, the proof encoding,
which predicates exist and the price of every exit.

\paragraph{One node format.} A parent hashes each child's sum, not only
their total, and this is a soundness property. A tree hashing $h_L \,\|\,
h_R \,\|\, (s_L + s_R)$ admits inflation: two leaves of 100 under a root of
sum 100 each have a proof whose sibling claims sum zero, and together the
proofs claim 200. Here the two proofs hash the distinct preimages $(A,
100, B, 0)$ and $(A, 0, B, 100)$, so at most one reaches the root; in Lean
this is \lean{serializeNode\_inj} with \lean{hNode\_inj}, from which
\lean{fold\_sound} gives that a verified leaf carries its true amount.

\paragraph{What the profiles decide.} A sparse position is a function of
the leaf, so a joiner computes it alone, a key and nonce cannot appear
twice, the root depends only on the leaf set, and absence is an
inclusion proof of $\Emp_0$. None of LayerTree's predicates needs these
properties: an exit proves inclusion and a join proves its slot is
$\Emp_h$, the same way in both profiles. The dense profile has aligned
subtrees instead, on which \tr{JOIN}$_h$, \tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$ and
every shape of \S\ref{sec:batching} depend.

\paragraph{Where the bytes go.} A sparse proof is the smaller witness and
the larger script, and the script wins (Table~\ref{tab:sizes}: about
$1{,}781\vB$ at $D = 32$ and $2^{16}$ members against $1{,}020\vB$ dense
at $D = 16$). Both causes follow from hashing the position. Random positions collide, $N^2/2^{D+1}$
expected collisions among $N$ members (about one half at $D = 32$ for $N
= 2^{16}$, about 32{,}000 at $D = 16$), so the sparse tree needs about
twice the depth, and every level is unrolled. And a sparse level is 135
bytes against 88, because it derives the direction bit from the
position, substitutes $\Emp_k$ for an elided sibling and computes
$\Emp_{k+1}$ for the next level. The full-width tree of Taproot
Assets~\cite{mssmt} ($D = 256$) costs $35{,}611$ bytes of script per
exit, about 17 times the dense script and 9 times the dense transaction;
MID caps sparse depth at 31.

Dense is therefore the default (spec~5.4, \path{spec/layertree.md}):
1.7 to 2.8 times cheaper per exit at $2^{16}$ members, cohort grafts at one price, sweeps at about $160\vB$ per leaf, and
every batch shape, at the price of an operator who keeps the slot map.
The sparse profile is kept, specified and tested, as the measured
alternative and for deployments that are key-addressed registries; it
still hosts channel leaves and vouchers and still exits in one
transaction.

\paragraph{Radix.} A radix-$r$ path carries $41(r-1)D/\log_2 r$ sibling
bytes, $41D$ at radix 2 and $61.5D$ at radix 4, so radix 2 minimises the
witness and a higher radix can only save the fixed part of the unrolled
level. Table~\ref{tab:radix} measures it. Of the radix-4 level's 146 bytes about 62
are hashing and sums shared with radix 2 and the rest sibling and
direction handling, so the fixed part a higher radix amortises is about
60 bytes while the sibling part is what it multiplies; radix 4 comes out
about 2\% larger overall. In the sparse profile most siblings are empty and cost
one witness byte, so a radix-4 level of about 180 bytes could replace two
levels of 135, saving about $360\vB$ per exit (estimate; no sparse
radix-$r$ level has been written). With a native Merkle-sum opcode a
level costs only its sibling and radix 2 is optimal.
