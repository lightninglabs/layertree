\section{Batching}\label{sec:batching}

The path is most of a transition's cost, about $510\vB$ of a $1{,}020\vB$
exit at $D = 16$, so batching is the question of how many leaves can
share one. A transition has a fixed part (skeleton, state binding,
epilogue, control block; about $470\vB$) paid once per transaction, about
$32\vB$ per shared level paid once per path, and about 100 to $120\vB$
per leaf touched (its 116 bytes, signature, payout and hashing script),
which no shape removes (all three are estimates from the measured
sizes). Batching therefore helps only leaves under one aligned subtree,
which cohort grafts create and the sparse profile cannot.

\paragraph{What exists.} \tr{EXIT}$_K$ chains single-leaf steps, each
with its full path, and saves only the skeleton ($665\vB$ for a second
exit). \tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$ is the real batch: the witness carries the
path above an aligned subtree once and every position of the subtree,
the script folds the subtree in post order checking each lease and
evictor key, and folds the result and $\Emp_h$ up the shared path
($158\vB$ per leaf for 51 leaves).

\paragraph{The cohort rewrite, \tr{REWRITE}$_h$.} Generalising the sweep
gives the general cohort transition. For each position of an aligned
subtree the witness carries an old and a new element (leaf or empty
marker); the old root, folded from the old elements, must fold with the
shared path to $(r, S)$; the new root is folded from the new elements;
output~0 is $(S - \sum\mathrm{old} + \sum\mathrm{new}, \TreeSpk(r', m))$,
an increase funded by other inputs. The owner of each changed leaf signs,
the operator signs once, and a leaf replaced by $\Emp_0$ must be paid to
its exit key from the next unused output, which makes the rewrite a safe
mass exit and subsumes the owner multiproof of spec 13.4. In one
transaction a cohort can then exit, join, resize, renew, rotate keys and
pay between members with the sum conserved, with only members whose
balances fall signing: the settlement step of an Ark round without a
round, an expiry, or unchanged members online. Exits cost the sweep plus
about 64 bytes of signature and 10 of script per leaf, 353, 222 and
$177\vB$ per leaf for 4, 13 and 51 owners, about 1.1 times the operator's
sweep; a changed leaf costs about $120\vB$ and an unchanged one $57\vB$
(estimates). The Lean model covers it as one \lean{Step} whose old and new
nodes are published subtrees: \lean{Steps.sum} gives conservation and
\lean{Steps.sound} grounding from the witness, so a cohort that joins
this way cannot be opaque.

\paragraph{Caching the path, \tr{DETACH}$_h$.} Script cannot read a
previous witness, so the upper levels of a cohort's path can only be
cached in an output of its own. \tr{DETACH}$_h$, signed by the operator,
proves an aligned subtree's node $(R_h, s_h)$ against $(r, S)$, leaves
$\Emp_h$ in output~0 for $S - s_h$, and creates output~1 as a depth-$h$
tree with root $R_h$, amount $s_h$ and a taptree $m_h$ fixed at creation;
members' paths truncated to the lower $h$ levels are their new proofs. At
$D = 16$, $h = 8$ it costs about $815\vB$ and every later exit $762\vB$
instead of $1{,}020\vB$ (estimates), repaid by the third exit. The larger
effect is throughput: the cohort's exits form their own chain of
unconfirmed spends, so $\lat(N)$ applies to it separately, and the
operator can shard on demand when, say, channel leaves near their HTLC
deadlines. Vouchers bind $m$ and must be reissued under $m_h$; channel
leaves need nothing, as their $\mathsf{chan\_id}$ fixes $m$ at creation
and a detach leaves every leaf's bytes unchanged. The Lean model needs a
two-output step to cover it.
