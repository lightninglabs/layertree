\section{The UC Proof}\label{app:uc}

This \thispart{} gives the simulator and the hybrid argument behind
Theorem~\ref{thm:uc}. We follow the conventions of the composable ledger
treatment of Badertscher et al.~\cite{bmtz17} and of Generalized
Channels~\cite{genchannels}: the ideal functionality itself has access to
the global ledger and posts the transactions that move coins, so that the
ideal and real worlds agree on what the ledger contains and differ only in
how the tree state is maintained.

\subsection{The Hybrid World}

The real protocol $\Pi_\tau$ runs in the presence of:

\begin{description}
\item[{$\Gledger[\Pred_\tau]$}] the global ledger functionality of
  Badertscher et al., parameterised by a validity predicate that, for a
  transaction spending a tree output, evaluates the tier's covenant
  predicate $\mathsf{Pred}_\tau$ on the input state, the witness and the
  outputs (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). It guarantees persistence, and liveness
  with parameter $\Delta_L$: a valid transaction with sufficient fee that
  an honest party submits is in the ledger state of every honest party
  within $\Delta_L$ rounds. The adversary chooses the order of
  transactions within the rules and may delay a submitted transaction by
  up to $\Delta_L$.
\item[$\Gclock$] the global clock of Katz et al.~\cite{kmtz13}; rounds are
  blocks.
\item[$\Fsig$] the ideal signature functionality~\cite{canettiSig}, which
  we use in place of an EUF-CMA reduction; every \texttt{CHECKSIG} and
  \texttt{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK} in the scripts is a call to its verification
  interface.
\end{description}

Hashes are treated in the idealised model of \S\ref{sec:security}
(injectivity plus tag separation); the simulator's abort events are
exactly the collision events this model excludes, so the argument
transfers to a collision-resistance reduction in the standard way.

\subsection{The Simulator}

$\Sim$ runs an internal copy of every honest party's protocol state and of
the adversary $\Adv$, and interposes between the environment, the ideal
functionality $\Ftree$ and the hybrids. Its behaviour is driven by two
kinds of events.

\paragraph{Ledger events.} Whenever a transaction $\mathsf{tx}$ spending a
tree output appears in the ledger (honest or adversarial), $\Sim$ parses
its witness and translates it into $\Ftree$ calls:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the script leaf executed from the control block; this fixes
  the transition type and its parameters ($K$, $h$, profile, variant).
\item For each step in the witness, run the fold on the supplied path and
  node. Because $\mathsf{tx}$ is valid, the old node folds to the
  committed root (\textsf{CHECK\_OLD} or \textsf{CHECK\_PREV}). By
  Theorem~\ref{thm:binding} (\lean{fold\_sound}), $\Sim$ recovers the unique
  position $p$ of that node in its copy of the tree $T$, or detects that
  no such position exists, which is a collision (abort event
  $\mathsf{Coll}$).
\item Map the step to the interface: a leaf node replaced by $\Emp_0$
  with the owner-flag clear is $\mathsf{EXIT}(p, \mathit{payout})$, with
  the flag set $\mathsf{EVICT}(p)$; $\Emp_h$ replaced by a node is
  $\mathsf{JOIN}(p, u)$ for a subtree $u$ that $\Sim$ obtains from the
  adversary's join messages (or records as ungrounded, see below); a leaf
  replaced by a leaf is $\mathsf{UPDATE}$; the two-step pattern of
  \S\ref{sec:protocol} is $\mathsf{CLAIM}$; the post-order pattern is
  $\mathsf{SWEEP}$.
\item Verify with $\Fsig$ that the authorising signatures are valid for
  the keys the leaf names. A valid signature for an honest party's key that
  the honest party did not produce is a forgery (abort event
  $\mathsf{Forge}$), which $\Fsig$ makes impossible.
\item Invoke the interface on $\Ftree$ on behalf of the party that
  authorised the transition (the corrupt party, or, for honest parties'
  own transactions, by letting the honest party's ideal instruction
  through).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Functionality events.} When $\Ftree$ executes an interface for
an honest party, $\Sim$ constructs the corresponding real transaction from
its copy of the honest state (leaf, path, signature via $\Fsig$) and
submits it to $\Gledger$ on the honest party's behalf, scheduling it
within the delay $\Ftree$ permits. $\Ftree$'s payouts are the ledger
outputs of that transaction, so the two worlds agree on the ledger.

\paragraph{Ungrounded grafts.} If a corrupt operator grafts a node whose
preimage tree $\Sim$ cannot obtain, $\Sim$ records the slot as
\emph{opaque} in $\Ftree$ (the functionality's $\mathsf{JOIN}$ accepts an
opaque node of the stated sum). Opaque slots contribute their sum to $A$
and can be swept or replaced but never exited; this is the real behaviour
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ground}), and an honest member never funds a join into
an opaque slot because $\Pi$ verifies its own path before funding.

\subsection{Hybrids}

\begin{description}
\item[$\mathcal{H}_0$] the real execution of $\Pi_\tau$ with $\Adv$ in
  the $(\Gledger, \Gclock, \Fsig)$-hybrid world.
\item[$\mathcal{H}_1$] as $\mathcal{H}_0$, but the execution aborts on
  $\mathsf{Forge}$. Indistinguishable by the definition of $\Fsig$.
\item[$\mathcal{H}_2$] as $\mathcal{H}_1$, but the execution aborts on
  $\mathsf{Coll}$: a valid witness whose old node is not the node at any
  position of the honest tree. By Theorem~\ref{thm:binding}, in the
  idealised hash model $\mathsf{Coll}$ has probability zero; under
  collision resistance it yields two preimages of $\Hnode$ or $\Hleaf$
  with equal digests.
\item[$\mathcal{H}_3$] as $\mathcal{H}_2$, but the honest parties' tree
  state is replaced by $\Ftree$'s and their transactions are produced by
  $\Sim$ from functionality events. We argue the two are identical
  transaction by transaction: for every accepted spend, step 2 of $\Sim$
  finds the position and Theorem~\ref{thm:ground} (\lean{Step.sound}) gives
  that the new root is the root of $T$ with that subtree replaced, so
  $\Ftree$'s state after the interface call equals the honest parties'
  real state. Conservation (\lean{Steps.sum}) gives that the output
  amounts $\Ftree$ pays are those the transaction carries; the
  real-tree invariant (\lean{Steps.sound}) gives that $\Ftree$'s $T$ is
  a tree whenever the real root is; and payability (\lean{payable}) gives
  that whenever $\Ftree$ executes an honest $\mathsf{EXIT}$, $\Sim$ can
  produce a valid real transaction. The ledger and clock are shared, so
  the environment's view of them is unchanged.
\item[$\mathcal{H}_4$] the ideal execution of $\Ftree$ with $\Sim$.
  Identical to $\mathcal{H}_3$ by construction.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Liveness.} $\Ftree$ permits $\Sim$ to delay an honest
$\mathsf{EXIT}$ or $\mathsf{EVICT}$ by up to $\Delta(N)$. In the real
world an honest exit competes for the tree output with at most $N - 1$
other transitions, of which at most $\kappa$ (the ancestor limit, 25 transactions
per unconfirmed chain) can be pending per confirmed output; an adversary who front-runs
maximally therefore delays the honest transaction by at most
$\lceil N / \kappa \rceil$ confirmations of the tree output, each within
$\Delta_L$, giving $\Delta(N) = \Delta_L \cdot (\lceil N/\kappa \rceil + 1)$.
This bound assumes the honest party pays a fee sufficient for
$\Gledger$'s liveness guarantee; the pinning discussion of
\S\ref{sec:security} is what happens when it does not.

\subsection{Per-Tier Remarks}

The argument is the same for every tier because the predicates are the
same; what differs is which predicates the tier realises
(Table~\ref{tab:tiers}) and one detail each of the MIN and CCV tiers. In MIN,
\textsf{CHECK\_OLD} and \textsf{FINAL} are enforced through the
constructive signature check of \S\ref{sec:min}, whose binding rests on
collision resistance of the TapSighash and challenge hashes rather than
on $\Gledger$'s predicate evaluation; $\mathcal{H}_2$ therefore also
aborts on a collision in those hashes, and $\Sim$ recovers the enforced
fields from the witness's raw pieces rather than from \texttt{OP\_TX}
selectors. In CCV, the witness carries neither $r$ nor $m$: the
input's commitment to the old root and output~0's commitment to the new
one are checked by \op{CHECKCONTRACTVERIFY} under the input's own
taptree, so the step of $\Sim$ that reads $r$ and $m$ from the witness
reads them from the spent output instead, and the binding of
\lean{witness\_root\_binds} rests on the BIP-443 data tweak in place of
$\TreeSpk$, with the same reduction to the discrete logarithm of
$\NUMS$ and collisions in the tweak hash. Its interfaces are FULL's,
with \iface{CLAIM} only in its \tr{SPLIT} form, under exact payouts.

\subsection{Composition for Channel Leaves}

Theorem~\ref{thm:chan} follows the pattern of Generalized
Channels~\cite{genchannels}, in the hybrid model with $\Ftree$, $\Gledger$,
$\Gclock$ and $\Fsig$. The
channel simulator reuses the channel-level simulator of that work for
the update and settlement transactions, which are ordinary ledger
transactions once the funding output exists, and uses $\Ftree$'s
$\mathsf{EXIT}$ and $\mathsf{EVICT}$ interfaces to materialise it. The
three tree-layer preconditions of \S\ref{sec:channels} are exactly the
conditions under which $\Ftree$ guarantees that the materialised output
carries the full capacity to the channel key: exact payouts
(\lean{ExitK.exact\_conservation}), non-claimability (the
\lean{SEQ\_FINAL} facts), and a lease at or below the tip so that
$\mathsf{EVICT}$ is available to the counterparty. We give this as a
sketch and leave the full channel-level proof, in particular the HTLC
deadline argument under $\Delta(N)$, to future work.
