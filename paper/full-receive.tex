\section{Receiving over Lightning}\label{sec:receive}

A user with no on-chain funds but a fee coin receives a Lightning payment and wants a
balance it can leave with alone. An Ark client gets an out-of-round
output co-signed by the operator that expires with its round, or, below
the operator's floor, a ledger credit with no on-chain claim. The tree
takes the same payment with less trust, through a \emph{cumulative}
voucher (Appendix~\ref{app:claim}): since a redemption advances the
source leaf's $\mathsf{seq}$ and voids every other voucher against it,
repeated receives are one voucher that the issuer re-signs for the
running total against the same claim leaf. The recipient keeps the
latest and needs no contest window, since it never prefers a smaller
state. The covenant stops the issuer from redeeming more than the source
holds; it does not stop over-issuance or voiding (advancing
$\mathsf{seq}$, or redeeming an older, smaller voucher, before the
recipient redeems). Both are closed by the source leaf's shape.

\paragraph{Atomic receive with pending leaves.}\label{sec:pending}
Settling first still leaves the recipient exposed for one payment. The
tree closes the gap with three added script variants, none needing an
opcode outside its tier's set. \emph{R1}: a leaf whose evictor is a
separate reclaim key can, after its deadline, be removed by the operator
alone with no payout. \emph{R2}: a hash-locked voucher (\tr{CLAIM}$^{hm}$)
that raises the source leaf's sibling to an absolute level and lowers the
source by the difference without advancing its $\mathsf{seq}$; the
sibling must be owned by the source's exit key and exit to it. \emph{R3}:
a \tr{REWRITE}$_h$ that moves value from a reclaimable leaf into that
sibling with the operator's signature alone. A recipient holds a pair, a
pending leaf the operator owns, naming the recipient's key as exit key
and the reclaim key as evictor, next to the recipient's own landing leaf.
The operator signs the merge voucher before the recipient releases the
preimage. The voucher can only move value from the pending leaf into a
leaf the recipient alone can exit, and the level it names is absolute, so
an older voucher redeemed after a newer moves nothing and one voucher
covers a whole window. A window ends in one of three ways: the operator
settles it with an R3 rewrite, a tower redeems the voucher before the
deadline, or the operator reclaims the pending leaf after it (R1) and
only then fails any inbound HTLC whose voucher is outstanding. The
recipient's liveness duty is one check of its landing leaf per window,
which a tower performs holding no key of the recipient's. The model of
Appendix~\ref{app:lean} proves that a merge or a transfer moves value only
between leaves that exit to one key, that a reclaim removes only flagged
leaves past their deadline, and that replaying a voucher moves nothing. The
credit leaf remains the choice for a wallet that accepts one payment of
trust and wants no deadline.
