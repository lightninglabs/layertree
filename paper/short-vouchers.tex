\section{Vouchers}\label{sec:vouchers}

A voucher lets a member pay someone, or the operator pay a member who
receives from Lightning, with a signature alone. Nothing reaches the
chain when it is issued, and its holder can redeem it later without the
issuer or the operator. Vouchers fill the place of Ark's out-of-round
payments with a different trade. An Ark transfer is undone only if
sender and server collude; a voucher can be voided by its issuer alone,
or by the source's evictor once the lease passes, until its redemption
confirms. In exchange, a redeemed voucher is a leaf its holder exits
alone, and its eviction at the lease pays the holder on-chain, where an
expired Ark round forfeits to the operator; and the credit leaf
(\S\ref{sec:creditleaf}) removes the issuer's voiding for payments from
the operator.

\subsection{The Voucher and \tr{CLAIM}}\label{sec:voucher-def}

A voucher is a BIP-340 signature by the owner key of a \emph{source}
leaf over
$H_{\mathsf{voucher}}(\mathsf{tree\_id} \,\|\, \hat\ell_{\mathrm{src}} \,\|\, \ell_{\mathrm{claim}})$,
where $\hat\ell_{\mathrm{src}}$ is the source leaf without its amount
(spec 0.13.0), $\ell_{\mathrm{claim}}$ is the full leaf being paid (an
amount and the recipient's keys), and $\mathsf{tree\_id}$ is a 32-byte
constant fixed when the tree is created (Appendix~\ref{app:impl}). Anyone who holds the voucher can redeem
it with \tr{CLAIM}, which checks the signature with
\op{CHECKSIGFROMSTACK} and needs no other. Like \tr{SPLIT}, it is two
steps (Figure~\ref{fig:claim}). The first proves $\ell_{\mathrm{src}}$
against the committed root and rewrites it with its amount reduced by
the claim's and its $\mathsf{seq}$ raised by one, or empties the
position if the voucher draws the whole balance. The second proves a
position empty under the intermediate root and places
$\ell_{\mathrm{claim}}$ there. The total is unchanged.

Two rules bind every carve, by voucher or by \tr{SPLIT}. The claim leaf
keeps the source's evictor key and a lease no later than the source's,
so moving a balance cannot escape eviction, nor plant a leaf in a cohort
that the operator could never sweep. And neither the claim leaf nor a
non-zero residual may be below $\mathsf{DUST\_MIN}$, by default 330\,sat,
the P2TR dust threshold: a smaller leaf could leave only through a
payout that default policy does not relay, so a one-satoshi carve could
block its cohort's sweeps and grafts for the life of the tree.

\paragraph{Single use.} Replay is impossible because redemption
advances the source's $\mathsf{seq}$, which the voucher binds. The
amount is not bound, so an amount-only top-up of the source
(\S\ref{sec:ops}) voids nothing, and the covenant's sum check still
stops a voucher drawing more than the source holds. Two rules that no
script enforces carry the rest: a leaf's $\mathsf{seq}$ only ever
advances (an \tr{UPDATE} winding it back would revive old vouchers), and
no two leaves share an owner key and nonce, since a voucher binds its
source by content, not position; in the dense profile an issuer checks
that no duplicate of its source leaf exists before signing, since one
voucher would redeem against both (spec 10.3). Binding $\mathsf{tree\_id}$ rather than the
taptree root $m$ keeps a voucher out of other trees built with the same
parameters, and since a detached cohort inherits $\mathsf{tree\_id}$,
vouchers survive a \tr{DETACH}$_h$.

\subsection{Cumulative Vouchers}\label{sec:cumulative}

Every voucher against a source draws on the same leaf, and the first
redemption voids the rest. An issuer that pays one recipient repeatedly
therefore signs a \emph{cumulative} voucher for the running total against
the same claim leaf, and re-signs it for a larger amount at each payment:
2, then 5, then 9. Each payment is one signature off-chain, with no
on-chain cost, no minimum and no expiry. The recipient keeps only the
latest voucher and, if the issuer stops cooperating, redeems that one
alone; it never prefers a smaller one, so it needs no window in which
to contest an old state.

Settled credit is retired by advancing $\mathsf{seq}$. Once the issuer
pays the recipient another way, for instance with a leaf of the
recipient's own in the next cohort graft (under $3\vB$ per member), it
advances the source's $\mathsf{seq}$, which voids every outstanding
voucher against it: one \tr{UPDATE} per source leaf ($1{,}030\vB$), or
one \tr{REWRITE}$_h$ for a cohort of them (about $148\vB$ per leaf at
$h = 6$). Until then the issuer tracks every voucher it has signed as a
liability. Plain \tr{CLAIM} leaves a hole: anyone holding an old voucher
can redeem it, and the issuer holds them all, so redeeming the voucher
for 2 voids the one for 9. The credit leaf below closes it.

\subsection{What the Holder Trusts}\label{sec:holder-risk}

The covenant prevents over-\emph{redemption}: the first redeemer is
paid by consensus. It cannot prevent over-\emph{issuance}, or voiding:
the issuer can advance $\mathsf{seq}$ first, by \tr{CLAIM}, \tr{SPLIT}
or \tr{UPDATE}. So the holder of an ordinary voucher extends credit to
the issuer until it redeems or is paid. Eviction voids vouchers too:
once the source's lease passes, its evictor can pay the whole balance to
the source's exit key, normally the issuer's. A holder therefore treats
a voucher whose source lease can pass before a redemption would confirm
(within $\lat(q)$, \S\ref{sec:liveness}) as unsecured. A partial-balance
voucher also needs an empty position for its claim leaf, which the
operator controls, so a holder prefers a full-balance voucher.

\subsection{Hash-Locked Vouchers and Swaps}\label{sec:hashlock}

\tr{CLAIM}$^{h}$ adds a payment hash to the signed message, under its
own tag so that the lock cannot be stripped, and requires the 32-byte
preimage: the script hashes it into the message, so a wrong preimage
fails as a bad signature. The same hash can sit in a channel-leaf HTLC or
a Lightning invoice, but the pairing is an ordered exchange, not an
atomic swap: the issuer can void the voucher until the redemption
confirms, and a voucher has no deadline, since script can forbid a spend
before a height but not after one. Five rules (spec~9.6.2) order it.
(1) The party the HTLC pays draws the preimage. (2) The voucher resolves
second; no HTLC waits on a preimage that only a redemption would reveal.
(3) The issuer leaves the source's $\mathsf{seq}$ alone from the moment
an HTLC on the hash is offered to it until the voucher is redeemed or
the HTLC fails. (4) No script enforces rule~3, so the holder redeems as
soon as it learns the preimage and bears the issuer risk until that
confirms. (5) The holder first checks that the source's lease outlasts
the HTLC's expiry plus $\lat(q)$. Usually the HTLC settles off-chain, so
such a swap costs at most one \tr{CLAIM}$^{h}$; it is also how a newcomer
buys a leaf with Lightning funds.

\paragraph{Costs.} Issuing a voucher costs nothing on-chain. A
redemption proves two positions, so at $D = 16$ it costs more than an
exit: $1{,}617\vB$ for \tr{CLAIM}, $1{,}627\vB$ for \tr{CLAIM}$^{h}$ and
$1{,}635\vB$ for \tr{CLAIM}$^{x}$, against $1{,}586\vB$ for \tr{SPLIT},
on the model of \S\ref{sec:eval}, which charges a carve no payout output
(with one, add $43\vB$). Redemption is the fallback; normally vouchers
are re-signed off-chain and retired in batches.
