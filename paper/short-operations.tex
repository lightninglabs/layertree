\section{Operations}\label{sec:ops}

Every operation is one spend of the tree output through one tapleaf.
The tree output is input~0, and output~0 recreates it under the new root
and total. Payouts are outputs $1, \dots, K$, each paying exactly a
removed leaf's amount to its exit key; every other input and output is
free, for fees, change and anchors. The witness proves each changed
position with a \emph{step}: the old node folded up its sibling path
must give the committed $(r, S)$, and the new node folded up the same
path gives $(r', S')$; steps chain. Signatures cover all outputs. If the
last leaf leaves, output~0 is unconstrained. Costs are whole
transactions at $D = 16$ in FULL, on the model of \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Joining}\label{sec:parties}

\paragraph{\tr{JOIN}$_h$: grafting a cohort.} The operator inserts a
subtree of up to $2^h$ new leaves, built off-chain, into an empty aligned
slot of height $h$; $h = 0$ adds one leaf. \emph{Authorised by} the
operator key alone; no existing member signs or is affected.
\emph{Proves} that the slot held the empty node $\Emp_h$, and adds the new
subtree's root and sum. \emph{On chain} the tree output grows by the
subtree's sum, which other inputs must supply. The covenant sees a hash
and a sum, not the leaves, and walks only the $D - h$ levels above the
slot, so a larger cohort is a smaller transaction: $937\vB$ for one
leaf, $872\vB$ for four ($218$ each), $679\vB$ for 256 ($2.7$ each). A taptree carries one \tr{JOIN}$_h$
leaf per height it supports.

Because the covenant cannot look inside the graft, each joiner checks
it. A member funding its own leaf verifies, before signing its input over
all outputs, that the slot folds as empty to the current root, that its
leaf folds to the grafted node with every sum recomputed, and that
output~0 commits to the new root; its input can then only be spent into
a root containing its leaf. A self-funded cohort is thus a signing
round, like an Ark round or a coinjoin, and each member adds its funding
input ($58\vB$). In an operator-funded graft the member pays off-chain,
after checking its path against the confirmed root, or through a
hash-locked voucher (\S\ref{sec:hashlock}).

\paragraph{\tr{JOINK}$_K$: batched single joins.} $K$ single-leaf joins
in sequence under one operator signature. It is the batching primitive of
the sparse profile, where a leaf's position is derived from its key and
cannot be grafted as part of a subtree, and costs $1{,}494\vB$ for two
in the dense profile.

\subsection{Moving Value}

Most payments never touch the tree. Members with channel leaves pay over
Lightning through the operator (\S\ref{sec:channels}), and vouchers
(\S\ref{sec:vouchers}) move value with a signature that is redeemed
only if the issuer stops cooperating. The tree changes when a balance's
terms change or a carve is redeemed.

\paragraph{\tr{UPDATE} and \tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$: changing a leaf.} Replaces
one leaf with another at the same position: a splice in or out, a lease
renewal, a key rotation or a transfer of ownership. \emph{Authorised by}
the leaf's owner and the operator together; \tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$, a variant
chosen at creation, adds the leaf's exit key as a third signer.
\emph{Proves} the old leaf against the root and writes the new one.
\emph{On chain} the tree output changes by the difference: an increase is
funded by other inputs, a decrease is free for the transaction to spend,
and both signers see where it goes. $1{,}030\vB$, or $1{,}048\vB$ with
the third signature. \tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$ exists for leaves whose exit key
is shared with or owned by someone else (a channel's counterparty, a
credit leaf's recipient, \S\ref{sec:creditleaf}): without it the owner
and the operator could splice such a leaf down together.

\paragraph{\tr{SPLIT}: paying a member directly.} Carves a new leaf out
of the payer's leaf. \emph{Authorised by} the payer's owner key, signing
this transaction. \emph{Proves} two positions in sequence: the source
leaf, rewritten with its amount reduced by the payment and its
$\mathsf{seq}$ raised by one (or emptied if drained), then an empty
position under the intermediate root, which receives the new leaf.
\emph{On chain} the total is unchanged and no payout is made. The new
leaf keeps the source's evictor key and a lease no later than the
source's, and neither it nor a non-zero residual may be below
$\mathsf{DUST\_MIN}$ (both rules are shared with \tr{CLAIM},
\S\ref{sec:voucher-def}). $1{,}586\vB$. \tr{SPLIT} is the right shape
when the payer is the one broadcasting and paying the fee; it leaves no
outstanding promise behind, and it is the one member-to-member transfer
the MIN tier supports. Its deferred counterpart, a voucher redeemed by
\tr{CLAIM}, is the subject of \S\ref{sec:vouchers}.

\subsection{Settling a Cohort}

\paragraph{\tr{REWRITE}$_h$: everything at once.} Replaces the contents
of one aligned subtree of height $h$ in a single spend: any position may
go from a leaf to empty (an exit, paid out), from empty to a leaf (a
join, funded) or from one leaf to another (a payment, renewal, resize or
key rotation). \emph{Authorised by} the operator and by the owner of
every leaf that changes; a leaf that only gains amount needs no
signature unless its \textsf{seq} is \textsf{SEQ\_FINAL} (a channel leaf,
\S\ref{sec:channels}, whose capacity its signed states fix), so a payment
within a cohort needs only the payer's, and a leaf nobody signs for passes
through unchanged. \emph{Proves} the old
subtree, every leaf of it published, against the root, and computes the
new one. \emph{On chain} each emptied leaf is paid exactly its amount at
its exit key, so the operator cannot use the rewrite to remove a leaf
without its owner's signature and its payout. At $h = 6$ a mass exit
costs $161\vB$ per leaf, about what a sweep costs the operator, and
rewriting every balance $147\vB$ per leaf against $1{,}030\vB$ for an
\tr{UPDATE} each. \tr{REWRITE}$_h^{x}$ adds the exit key's signature
($16\vB$ per signed position) and replaces \tr{REWRITE}$_h$ wherever
\tr{UPDATE}$^{x}$ or \tr{CLAIM}$^{x}$ is present. FULL only.

\subsection{Leaving}

\paragraph{\tr{EXIT}$_K$: unilateral exit.} Removes one leaf, or $K$
leaves in one transaction, and pays each out
(Figure~\ref{fig:exit}). \emph{Authorised by} the leaf's owner key alone;
no operator, no other member, no timelock. \emph{Proves} the leaf and its
path against the root and folds the empty node up the same path for the
new root. \emph{On chain} the tree output shrinks by the leaf's amount
and output~1 pays its exit key. In FULL the payout may be less than the
balance, the difference paying the fee, which saves the fee input
($99\vB$); the exact-payout variant \tr{EXIT}$_K^{=}$, chosen at
creation and visible in the tree's taptree root, requires the full
amount and is what MID, MIN, CCV and channel leaves use. $1{,}020\vB$ for
one exit, and $665\vB$ more for a second in the same transaction
(\tr{EXIT}$_2$ is $1{,}684\vB$). The owner's signature commits to the
tree outpoint, which every operation changes, so an exit cannot be
pre-signed: the owner must be online to rebuild it after a competing
spend. Owners first ask for a cooperative exit, which the operator can
batch.

\subsection{Being Evicted}

\paragraph{Eviction by \tr{EXIT}.} A lease is how the operator recovers
its capital and slots from a member who has gone away, and eviction is
the only way it can remove a leaf without the owner. The exit script,
with a flag, removes a leaf whose lease has passed. \emph{Authorised by} the leaf's evictor
key, with a lock time at or after the lease height. \emph{On chain} the
payout is exactly the leaf's amount, to the leaf's own exit key, so an
eviction returns the member's money rather than taking it. Same cost as
an exit; the evictor pays the fee.

\paragraph{\tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$: the cohort sweep.} Evicts every
remaining leaf of one aligned subtree in one spend. \emph{Authorised by}
the operator key, which must be every swept leaf's evictor, with a lock
time past every lease. \emph{Proves} the subtree from all its leaves
(empty positions contribute $\Emp_0$), each internal node hashed once,
against the root. \emph{On chain} one exact payout per leaf, and the
subtree becomes empty. $203\vB$ per leaf for 13 leaves at $h = 4$ and
$158\vB$ per leaf for 51 at $h = 6$, against $1{,}020\vB$ each alone.
One leaf with a later lease holds up its cohort's sweep until that lease
passes, and one with another evictor blocks it, so operators admit only
leaves that name the operator as evictor into a cohort, align leases per
cohort, and renew a member by moving it to a cohort with the new lease
(an \tr{UPDATE} followed by a \tr{SPLIT} of the whole balance).

\subsection{Scaling Out}

\paragraph{\tr{DETACH}$_h$: a cohort's own output.} Moves one aligned
subtree into a tree output of its own. \emph{Authorised by} the operator
key. \emph{Proves} the subtree's root and sum against the parent root.
\emph{On chain} output~0 is the parent minus the cohort and output~1 the
cohort, under its own root and the parent's rules compiled at depth $h$;
the detached tree inherits the parent's tree identifier, so vouchers and
channel states against its leaves stay valid. No member needs new data:
its proof in the detached tree is its old path cut to the lower $h$
levels. $780\vB$ at $h = 8$, after which every exit from the cohort costs
$762\vB$ instead of $1{,}020$; the detach pays for itself at the fourth
exit. Its larger effect is throughput, since each output is spent
independently (\S\ref{sec:liveness}).
