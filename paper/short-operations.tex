\section{Operations}\label{sec:ops}

Every operation is one spend of the tree output through one tapleaf, its
\emph{predicate} (Figure~\ref{fig:compose}): the tree output is input~0,
output~0 recreates it under the new root and total, payouts are outputs
$1, \dots, K$, and every other input and output is free. The witness
proves each changed position with a \emph{step}: the old node folded up
its sibling path gives the committed $(r, S)$, the new node folded up the
same path gives $(r', S')$, and steps chain. Costs are whole transactions
at $D = 16$ in FULL (\S\ref{sec:eval}). The four operations a member meets
most are stated as predicate boxes and a member's side of leaving and
joining as algorithms; Appendix~\ref{app:ops} gives who authorises each
operation and what it costs, and the full version states every predicate
and every party.

\subsection{Joining}\label{sec:parties}

\tr{JOIN}$_h$ grafts a subtree of up to $2^h$ new leaves, built off-chain,
into an empty aligned slot on the operator's signature alone. The covenant
sees a hash and a sum and walks only the $D - h$ levels above the slot, so
a larger cohort is a smaller transaction: $937\vB$ for one leaf, $872\vB$
for four, an estimated $679\vB$ for 256. Since it cannot look inside the
graft, each joiner verifies it (Algorithm~\ref{alg:accept}).

\subsection{Moving Value}

Most payments never touch the tree: channel leaves pay over Lightning
(\S\ref{sec:channels}) and vouchers (\S\ref{sec:vouchers}) move value with a
signature. The tree changes by \tr{UPDATE} (the owner and the operator,
$1{,}030\vB$) or when a carve is redeemed. A carve writes a new leaf out of
a source leaf's balance in two steps and conserves the total: \tr{CLAIM}
on a voucher, \tr{SPLIT} on the owner's transaction signature
($1{,}586\vB$). Checks~7 and~8 bind every carve: the new leaf keeps the
source's evictor and a lease no later, so a balance cannot escape eviction,
and neither it nor a residual may fall below $\mathsf{DUST\_MIN}$, which
default policy could not relay a payout for.

\predClaim

\subsection{Settling a Cohort}

\tr{REWRITE}$_h$ replaces the contents of one aligned subtree in a single
spend: a position may go from a leaf to empty (an exit, paid out), from
empty to a leaf (a join) or from one leaf to another. The operator and the
owner of every changing leaf authorise it, but a leaf that only gains
amount needs no signature, so a payment within a cohort needs only the
payer's. At $h = 6$ a mass exit costs $162\vB$ per leaf and rewriting every
balance $148\vB$, against $1{,}030\vB$ for an \tr{UPDATE} each. The reclaim
form (check~8) serves the receive of \S\ref{sec:receive-short}.

\predRewrite

\subsection{Leaving}\label{sec:leaving}

\tr{EXIT}$_K$ removes one leaf, or $K$ in one transaction, and pays each
out on the owner's signature alone: no operator, no other member, no
timelock. It costs $1{,}020\vB$, and $665\vB$ more for a second leaf. The
signature commits to the tree outpoint, which every operation changes, so
a plain exit cannot be pre-signed and the owner must be online to rebuild
it after a competing spend. The tower variant $\tr{EXIT}^{t}_K$ signs the
leaf without its amount, checked from the stack, so a tower builds and
rebuilds the exit with its own fee input; the owner voids it by changing
the leaf, and draws a fresh nonce at every join (spec~9.2).

\paragraph{Eviction and scaling out.} The exit predicate with its evict
flag removes a leaf whose lease has passed, on the evictor's signature, and
pays the leaf's whole amount to its exit key; \tr{EVICT\_SUBTREE}$_h$
sweeps an aligned subtree in one spend at $158\vB$ per leaf for 51 leaves
at $h = 6$, against $1{,}020\vB$ each alone. \tr{DETACH}$_h$ moves a cohort
into a tree output of its own ($780\vB$ at $h = 8$, then $762\vB$ per
exit), which raises throughput, since each output is spent independently
(\S\ref{sec:liveness}).
