# Review: protocol engineer profile

**Paper:** "LayerTree: A Shared Bitcoin UTXO as an Ideal Functionality, and Its Realisation with Covenants", `paper/main.pdf` built at commit `6d2c114` (short version), read with the `paper/*.tex` sources at `cb81f99`.
**Reviewer profile:** Bitcoin protocol engineer (Lightning implementation, covenant proposal review, tapscript limits, mempool policy).
**Scope:** scripts, opcode assumptions, deployment and policy claims, cost model, comparisons. The first-round items in `paper/review.md` (tier coverage, MIN binding argument, comparison model, varops fractions, HTLC budget formula) are treated as settled unless the fix regressed.

## Summary verdict

The construction is real engineering: I re-derived the 946 WU skeleton, the rounding of every vB figure from `spec/scripts/sizes.md`, the MID stack bound, and ran `REWRITE_6` in the simulator (peak stack 224 elements, about 37M varops, comfortably inside any plausible BIP-440 budget), and the large scripts are not where the trouble is. The trouble is in three places a node implementer would push on first. (1) The liveness parameter Delta(N), which the UC theorem and the channel composition both consume, rests on the claim that an honest exit competes with at most N - 1 other transitions; that is false, because `CLAIM`, `SPLIT`, `UPDATE` and `JOIN` let a single corrupt member (or the operator) generate an unbounded number of tree transitions without removing a leaf, and the kappa = 25 bound it multiplies is mempool policy that the ledger model does not contain, that the adversary can consume through the free outputs every transition carries, and that cluster mempool has since replaced. (2) The deployment story says "only proposals already made", but no CCV variant and not MID uses its opcodes in the leaf version their proposals define, FULL needs two drafts whose opcode numbers collide, one of those drafts is the author's own, and the paper lists none of the spec's assumptions [A-1] to [A-6]. (3) The closest prior art for single-transaction pool exit (TLUV, OP_MERKLESUB as used by CoinPool, OP_EVICT) and for the s = e + 1 generator-signature trick (Poelstra's CAT and Schnorr tricks) is missing, while the paper claims novelty for the latter. There are also two smaller but systematic problems: every vB figure omits the witness length prefixes (about 1% for FULL, more for the element-heavy 0xC0 tiers), and "membership changes need no interactive signing round" does not hold for the self-funded cohort joins the paper costs. **Recommendation: weak reject in the current form.** Findings 1 to 5 are fixable in text plus a restated theorem; none requires a new construction.

## Findings

### 1. The liveness bound Delta(N) is false as stated, and Theorem 5 inherits it

**Severity:** blocking.
**Location:** `paper/main.tex` 351 to 361 ("The liveness bound"); Theorem 5 (`thm:uc`, line 782); `paper/appendix-uc.tex` 126 to 135 ("Liveness").
**Claim:** "an honest exit competes for the tree output with at most N - 1 other transitions, of which at most kappa ... can be pending per confirmed output", giving Delta(N) = Delta_L(ceil(N/kappa) + 1).
**Problem.** The count N - 1 assumes every competing transition removes a corrupt member's leaf. It does not. With the reference script set (spec 6.2 includes `CLAIM`), one corrupt member with an ordinary leaf signs a voucher to itself, `CLAIM`s a 330-sat leaf into an empty slot, `EXIT`s it, and repeats; `seq` admits 2^32 iterations and the leaf count never falls. `SPLIT` (unilateral) does the same; a corrupt operator does it with `UPDATE` on its own leaf or `JOIN` then `EXIT` of a recycled dust leaf. So the adversary's supply of conflicting transitions is bounded only by its fee budget, not by N. Separately, kappa is not a ledger property: `G_ledger` in the Badertscher et al. model has no chain-length limit, and an adversarial scheduler (or miner) may place any number of its own chained transitions ahead of the honest one in a block. The appendix even calls kappa "the ancestor limit" while the body calls it the descendant limit.
**Resolution.** Either (a) restate Theorem 5 with Delta(N) as a function of the adversary's fee budget B and the honest party's fee f (the honest party wins as soon as it out-pays one block's worth of adversarial chain), and say plainly that unilateral exit is a fee auction against the adversary; or (b) restrict the adversary (honest operator and no corrupt member with `CLAIM`/`SPLIT`) for the liveness half only; or (c) change the design so transitions that do not remove value are rate-limited in script (for example, `CLAIM`/`SPLIT`/`UPDATE` carry `1 OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY`, so they can only spend a confirmed tree output and only removals can chain). In every case, model kappa explicitly as a parameter of the ledger functionality (at most kappa chained spends of one output per round) rather than importing it from policy, and make the appendix and body use one term.

### 2. kappa = 25 is the wrong policy number, the wrong citation, and adversary-controlled

**Severity:** major.
**Location:** `paper/main.tex` 354 to 361 ("25 under today's policy~\cite{bip125}"); `spec/layertree.md` 10.5; `spec/channel-leaves.md` C.9.5.
**Claim:** "if the ledger admits at most kappa unconfirmed descendants of an output (25 under today's policy [BIP-125], a shape cluster mempool changes but does not remove)".
**Problem.** (i) The 25-transaction limit is Bitcoin Core's ancestor/descendant count default; BIP-125 is the RBF signalling rule and says nothing about chain length. (ii) Cluster mempool has since been merged into Bitcoin Core and replaces the ancestor/descendant count limits with a per-cluster limit (64 transactions, 101 kvB by default); the binding constraint is now cluster count and size, which is shared by everything connected to the chain. (iii) Every transition leaves fee change, payouts and anchors as free outputs (Figure 2, "Everything else in the transaction is free"). Any participant can hang unconfirmed descendants off its own payout or change, or use an unconfirmed fee input with a long ancestry, and so consume the cluster budget of the whole tree chain; a member who exits once and attaches ~100 kvB of low-feerate descendants to its payout both caps the chain and makes its own exit expensive to replace (RBF must pay the absolute fees of everything evicted). The effective kappa is therefore chosen by the adversary and can be 1. (iv) Tree transitions cannot use TRUC (BIP-431) because TRUC caps an unconfirmed chain at two transactions, so the pinning protections the paper relies on for channel transactions are unavailable for the transitions themselves.
**Resolution.** Cite Bitcoin Core policy (and cluster mempool) rather than BIP-125; restate kappa under cluster limits; state that kappa is a lower bound only when no participant attaches descendants, and add descendant attachment to the pinning paragraph of "What the theorem leaves out" (line 817) alongside the low-feerate-ancestor case already there. If the authors want throughput claims of "25 exits per block", they must be stated as cooperative-case numbers.

### 3. "Only proposals already made" overstates the status of every tier except MIN

**Severity:** major.
**Location:** abstract (lines 143 to 148); `paper/main.tex` 198 to 207 ("We propose no opcode of our own"); Table 2 (`tab:tiers`, line 563); 601 to 602 ("which the draft leaves open"); 1074 to 1079; `paper/refs.bib` entries `ecops`, `optx`, `bip449`.
**Claim:** the four tiers are "built only from proposals already made for Bitcoin"; "We propose no opcode of our own."
**Problem.** Read against spec 4.1 and 4.5 and `tools/script_sim.py` lines 36 to 51:
- MID assumes `OP_TX` in tapleaf 0xC0 with `CScriptNum` outputs ([A-5]). The `OP_TX` draft is defined only for 0xC2 and uses BIP-440 varops accounting; a 0xC0 variant is not "left open", it does not exist.
- CCV in 0xC2 assumes BIP-443 in 0xC2, which BIP-443 does not define (it redefines a 0xC0 `OP_SUCCESS`); CCV in 0xC0 needs `OP_TX` in 0xC0, the same unproposed [A-5]. Neither CCV variant uses its opcodes where their proposals put them.
- FULL assumes CSFS and the EC opcodes in 0xC2 at stated costs ([A-2], [A-3]) and needs `OP_TX` (0xbd) together with `OP_EC_POINT_NEGATE` (0xbd); CCV (0xbb) collides with `OP_EC_POINT_ADD` (0xbb), and `OP_TWEAKADD` is listed as "0xBE or TBD" against `OP_EC_POINT_X_COORD` (0xbe). The simulator parks `OP_TX`, `OP_TWEAKADD` and CCV at placeholders 0xbf, 0xc0, 0xc1, so the "byte-exact golden scripts" are byte-exact against a numbering no proposal uses. Sizes are unaffected; the deployability claim is.
- The EC-ops draft (`ecops`, bitcoin/bips PR #1945) is authored by the paper's author. "We propose no opcode of our own" should disclose this, and under `\anontrue` the citation de-anonymises the submission.
- `OP_TX` is cited as a PR on a personal fork; `bip449` has no URL beyond the repository root and an unspecific author list.
- None of [A-1] to [A-6] appears in the paper (grep finds none). [A-1] matters most: FULL compares 8-byte little-endian `OP_TX` amounts with `OP_NUMEQUALVERIFY`, which works only if BIP-441 accepts non-minimal numeric operands; the paper never states this dependency.
**Resolution.** Add a one-table "deployment status" to section 5: per tier, per opcode, the proposal, its status (assigned BIP, draft PR, personal fork), the leaf version it is defined for, the leaf version the tier assumes, the opcode number assumed, and the pinned revision. Carry [A-1] to [A-6] into the paper verbatim. Say explicitly that MIN (BIP-347 `OP_CAT` plus BIP-449 `OP_TWEAKADD`, both 0xC0, if BIP-449 is a 0xC0 redefinition) is the only tier whose opcodes are all used as proposed, and that MID and both CCV variants need a cross-leaf-version port that nobody has proposed. Disclose authorship of the EC-ops draft.

### 4. Missing prior art for single-transaction pool exit, and a false novelty claim for the MIN trick

**Severity:** major.
**Location:** `paper/main.tex` 1029 to 1048 (Related Work), 1061 to 1062 ("We know of no prior use of a generator-key signature with s = e + 1"); Table 4 (`paper/appendix-eval.tex` 88 to 104); intro 166 to 176.
**Claim:** every prior shared-UTXO design makes exit a path of O(log N) transactions; CoinPool is "realised with pre-signed or covenant-committed transactions, so exit and update remain transaction trees", with opcodes "CTV or APO"; and the generator-key s = e + 1 construction is new.
**Problem.** (i) `OP_TAPLEAF_UPDATE_VERIFY` (A. Towns, bitcoin-dev, September 2021) was designed for exactly this: a joinpool member leaves in one transaction that recreates the pool output with its key and leaf removed, with `IN_OUT_AMOUNT` enforcing value, no expiry and no pre-signed interior. (ii) CoinPool v0.1's withdrawal design rests on `OP_MERKLESUB` and `SIGHASH_GROUP`/ANYPREVOUT for one-transaction withdrawal that recommits the remaining pool, not on CTV; please check Table 4's "O(log N) txs" and "CTV or APO" against the paper you cite. (iii) `OP_EVICT` (ZmnSCPxj, bitcoin-dev, February 2022) is a one-transaction eviction of unresponsive pool members. LayerTree's contribution over these is real (balances as a Merkle-sum tree rather than keys in a taptree, depth-independent eviction by lease, cohort grafts and sweeps, composition with channels), but the intro's framing that all prior designs pay O(log N) transactions at exit is not correct. (iv) The s = e + 1 signature under key G with nonce G, with grinding of a transaction field to avoid the carry, is A. Poelstra's "CAT and Schnorr Tricks I" (2021), and it has since been used in public CAT-covenant demonstrations (for example StarkWare's 2024 vault prototypes). The MIN tier's application (binding `TreeSPK` and the successor output through a constructed BIP-341 message) is a good use of it, not the first.
**Resolution.** Add TLUV, `OP_MERKLESUB`/CoinPool's withdrawal path, and `OP_EVICT` to Related Work and to Table 4, with a sentence on what LayerTree adds over each (sum tree, leases, grafting, channels, no new opcode). Replace the novelty sentence at line 1061 with a citation to Poelstra and a statement of what is new in the MIN use. Narrow the intro's "Every deployed or proposed approach" to the transaction-tree family it actually describes.

### 5. Self-funded cohort joins require an interactive signing round

**Severity:** major.
**Location:** abstract line 137 to 138 ("Membership changes need no interactive signing round"); G4 at `paper/main.tex` 342 to 344; `paper/main.tex` 537 to 540 (honest parties verify before funding); "Batching, joins and cohorts" (onboarding "dominated by the members' funding inputs (about 58 vB each)"); Table 4 row "Interactive signing: none".
**Claim:** JOIN and SWEEP "involve only the operator and the ledger".
**Problem.** The covenant needs only sigma_op, but the deposit does not. In a self-funded `JOIN_8` of 256 members each member's funding input is signed over a transaction whose output 0 commits to S + s_new and the new root; even `SIGHASH_ALL|ANYONECANPAY` commits to that output. The cohort must therefore be fixed before anyone signs, and one member who withdraws or never signs invalidates every other signature: this is the same coordination round, with the same denial-of-service exposure, as an Ark round or a coinjoin. The alternatives (operator fronts the liquidity and is repaid off-chain; per-member `JOIN_0` at about 2 kWU each) change the trust or cost story and are not what the paper measures.
**Resolution.** Restate G4 as "the covenant requires only the operator's signature"; say that a self-funded cohort graft needs every funder to sign one transaction after the cohort is fixed, with the abort-and-retry DoS that implies; and give the operator-fronted alternative and its liquidity cost. Adjust the abstract and Table 4 accordingly (covenant Ark has the same property, so the comparison is unaffected in sign).

### 6. Every vB figure omits witness length prefixes, and the spec says otherwise

**Severity:** major (mechanical fix).
**Location:** Table 3 (`paper/appendix-eval.tex` 13 to 31), every vB in sections 5 and 8, the fee tables; `tools/test_scripts.py` 131 to 132, `tools/test_ccv.py` 143 to 144, `tools/gen_listings.py` 74 to 77; `spec/layertree.md` section 11 ("each level costs 131 WU (88 script + 41 path + 2 length prefixes)").
**Claim:** dense `EXIT_1` at D = 16 is 3,132 WU of witness and 1,020 vB.
**Problem.** "Witness data" is computed as `sum(len(x) for x in wit)`, the raw element bytes, and "total witness" adds the script and a 129-byte control block with no serialisation overhead. A real witness also carries the item count, one CompactSize length per element, a three-byte length for the 2,101-byte script and one for the control block. For FULL `EXIT_1` at D = 16 the witness has 37 elements, so the omission is about 42 WU: about 1,031 vB, not 1,020. The measured per-level slope confirms it: (2,616 - 2,100) / 4 = 129 WU per level, not the 131 WU the spec says it counts. The error grows with element count, so it is larger for MID, MIN and CCV-0xC0 (seven-element leaves, `PADZ` pairs) and for the MID sweep (spec 4.5 gives about 950 witness elements at h = 6, roughly 240 vB), which biases the tier comparison slightly in favour of the 0xC0 tiers. Separately, the caption says "approximately 950 WU" while the fee tables use 946.
**Resolution.** Compute weight from a serialised transaction (the Go implementation already builds and signs every FULL transition; use `blockchain.GetTransactionWeight` on those), or add the prefixes in `gen_listings.py`, and regenerate Tables 2, 3 and the fee tables. Fix the "131 WU" sentence or the code so they agree. Use one skeleton figure (946 WU, with its composition: P2WPKH fee input, P2WPKH change, two P2TR outputs) everywhere.

### 7. Channel hosting: the 220-block budget and the "hub of N channels" do not fit together

**Severity:** major.
**Location:** abstract line 153 ("a hub of N channels occupies one output"); `paper/main.tex` 862 to 868 (G_exit = 72); Theorem 6 (`thm:chan`); `paper/appendix-channels.tex` 84 to 104; `spec/channel-leaves.md` C.4.4, C.7, C.9.5.
**Claim:** G_exit "is the time to get the exit confirmed including contention, that is, Delta(N)", and with G_exit = 72 the HTLC budget is about 220 blocks.
**Problem.** Delta(N) = Delta_L(ceil(N/25) + 1), so G_exit = 72 holds only for N up to about 1,775 channel leaves with Delta_L = 1 and no adversarial contention, yet the force-close table and every cost figure are at D = 16 (65,536 slots), and the paper itself says 10,000 leaves need about 400 blocks. In a channel leaf the counterparty is normally the operator, which is exactly the party with an interest in delaying the member's exit past an HTLC deadline, and by finding 1 it can generate competing transitions without bound; HTLC safety then reduces to winning a fee auction for the tree output. Two MID limits that bear on the hub claim appear only in an appendix: at most seven HTLCs per state (520-byte element limit on the settlement collation) and a 2^31-sat (21.47 BTC) cap per tree.
**Resolution.** State the sizing rule in the body (spec C.9.5 already has it: leaves / kappa < cltv_expiry_delta - csv_delay - 3 - G_sweep), give the maximum channel leaves per tree for G_exit = 72, and present the D = 16 force-close costs as costs for a tree of that occupancy. Say explicitly that against an operator-counterparty the budget holds only if the member can out-bid the operator's chain for one block (finding 1). Move the MID seven-HTLC and 21.47 BTC limits into section 7.

### 8. Exits cannot be pre-signed or delegated; the owner key must be online through contention

**Severity:** minor.
**Location:** `paper/main.tex` 519 to 520 ("Signatures must cover all outputs"), 532 to 545 (Honest Parties); Table 4 row "Interactive signing".
**Claim:** implicit: the member "builds the transaction of Figure 2" whenever it wants to exit.
**Problem.** The owner's `SIGHASH_DEFAULT` signature commits to the tree outpoint, which changes with every transition. An exit cannot be signed in advance, handed to a watchtower, or rebroadcast after any competing transition confirms; every retry during the Delta(N) window needs the owner key online, and so does every RBF bump. Ark's unilateral exit, by contrast, is a set of pre-signed transactions anyone can broadcast. For channel leaves on mobile wallets this is an availability requirement the paper should state.
**Resolution.** State it. Better, note the available fix: since the predicate already proves the leaf against the root and fixes output 0 and the exact payout, the owner's authorisation can be a prevout-independent `CHECKSIGFROMSTACK` signature over (m, leaf, payout) in FULL, MID and CCV, which makes the exit delegatable to a tower that supplies the fee input; under exact payouts nothing else in the transaction needs the owner's consent.

### 9. `CLAIM^h` is a hash-locked cheque, not an HTLC, and the issuer can win the race

**Severity:** minor.
**Location:** `paper/appendix-eval.tex` 54 to 72 ("turns a voucher into an HTLC"); `spec/layertree.md` 9.6.2 ("form an atomic swap").
**Claim:** the hash lock makes a voucher composable with a Lightning HTLC; the spec calls the pairing atomic.
**Problem.** The value never leaves the source leaf, and the issuer can void the voucher at any moment with a unilateral `EXIT` or `SPLIT` of that leaf, which conflicts with the redeemer's `CLAIM^h` on the tree outpoint. In the spec's own canonical flow the issuer collects the Lightning payment by releasing R and can then broadcast a higher-fee `SPLIT` that wins over the redeemer's pending redemption; rule 3 of 9.6.2 forbids this but no script enforces it. The appendix's "adds ordering, not security" is right; "HTLC" and "atomic swap" are not.
**Resolution.** Call it a hash-locked voucher or cheque, drop "atomic" from spec 9.6.2, and state the race in one sentence: the redeemer bears issuer risk until its `CLAIM^h` is confirmed at depth, and contention on the tree output (finding 1) lengthens that window.

### 10. MIN: the promised fee-bumping and grinding details are not in the appendix, and one witness element can be non-standard

**Severity:** minor.
**Location:** `paper/main.tex` 623 to 626 ("Appendix~\ref{app:scripts} has the enforced fields and the fee-bumping consequences"); `paper/appendix-scripts.tex` "The MIN message"; `tools/layertree_min.py` 148 to 158.
**Problem.** First-round item C asked for the `nSequence` range that avoids BIP-68 locks, for the fact that every fee bump requires re-grinding and re-signing, and for CPFP as the bump path; `review-response.md` records these as added, but the current appendix table says only "sha_sequences: raw, the field the spender grinds". Second, MIN passes all extra outputs as one raw witness element (`extra_outs`). Bitcoin Core policy limits 0xC0 tapscript witness items to 80 bytes (`MAX_STANDARD_TAPSCRIPT_STACK_ITEM_SIZE`), so two P2TR change outputs (86 bytes) make a MIN exit non-standard today; one P2TR change plus a P2A anchor (56 bytes) fits. The simulator models consensus limits (520 bytes, 1,000 elements) but not this policy limit.
**Resolution.** Restore the grinding range and fee-bump consequences in Appendix C; state the 80-byte limit on `extra_outs` (or split it into per-output elements); note in section 8 that the simulator does not model standardness rules for 0xC0 spends.

### 11. Comparison fairness: deployed Ark is missing, the tree-model parameters are unsourced, and "no expiry" needs its qualifier

**Severity:** minor.
**Location:** Table 4 (`paper/appendix-eval.tex` 88 to 104); "Comparison with transaction trees" (`paper/main.tex` 935 to 949); G5 (line 344); conclusion line 1093 ("no pre-signing, no expiry").
**Problem.** (i) Table 4's Ark column is "Ark (covenant)" with opcodes "CTV / CCV"; the Ark that runs on mainnet today uses pre-signed MuSig2 trees and needs no soft fork, which is the comparison a practitioner cares about (LayerTree's FULL tier needs BIP-440/441, `OP_TX`, CSFS and the EC-ops draft). (ii) The 150 vB per level and 111 vB claim, and the radix-2 and radix-4 choices, are asserted, not sourced from an implementation; deployed tree transactions are zero-fee with P2A anchors and need a CPFP child per level, and real round trees are sized by round population, not by a fixed D = 16. Compare at equal N, using a deployed implementation's radix and transaction sizes. (iii) "No expiry" is true of the shared output, but each member holds a lease whose renewal needs the operator's signature (`UPDATE`), so a member's off-chain position does expire at the operator's discretion. The difference from Ark is real and favourable (eviction pays the member on-chain at the operator's fee; Ark expiry lets the operator reclaim), and the paper should claim that difference rather than "no expiry".
**Resolution.** Add a deployed-Ark column, source the model's parameters, compare at equal N, and add a "member position expiry" row to Table 4 with the lease semantics stated for each design.

### 12. Smaller inconsistencies between paper, spec and appendix

**Severity:** nit.
- `paper/main.tex` 437 to 438, "Since every predicate checks input 0, two trees cannot be spent atomically", is false for CCV, whose tree input may sit at any index (spec 4.5, "Transaction shape"); qualify it.
- The CCV tier's liveness depends on BIP-443's amount semantics as of `bitcoin/bips` commit `24e96e87` (spec 4.1 item 7); the paper should cite the pinned commit, and state that conservation in CCV rests on the `OP_TX` equalities while the CCV amount rules can only make honest spends fail if BIP-443 changes.
- `spec/layertree.md` section 11 table gives `EVICT_SUBTREE_4` at about 165 vB per leaf and `EVICT_SUBTREE_6` at about 116, omitting the additional payout outputs; the paper's 203 and 158 are the correct whole-transaction figures. The same section's "breaks even near D = 5 ... about 2.4x" disagrees with the paper's D = 3 and 60%, and "sparse 1.6 to 2.7x" with the paper's 1.8 to 2.7.
- `spec/layertree.md` 10.5 still has "at most one confirms per tree per block position", removed from the paper in round one.
- `spec/layertree.md` 10.10 says no stack element exceeds 40 bytes; the FULL leaf is a 116-byte element and the node-hash preimage is 146 bytes.
- The EC-ops operand convention is written "[scalar] [point] with the scalar on top" in spec 4.1, the reverse of the bottom-to-top notation used for `OP_EC_POINT_ADD` one line earlier; pin it to a draft revision as is done for BIP-443.
