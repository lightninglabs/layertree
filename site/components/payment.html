Paying each other

Members pay each other off-chain: through Lightning channels that live inside leaves, or with vouchers, signed instructions to carve a new leaf out of the signer's balance. The tree changes only when a voucher is redeemed, and then one transition moves the value without changing the total.

Vouchers and CLAIM

A voucher is a message signed by a leaf's owner that names the tree, the owner's leaf (the source) and a new leaf (the claim). The tree is named by a 32-byte identifier fixed when it is created, so a voucher signed for one tree cannot be redeemed against another, even one whose leaves happen to match, and a cohort that is detached into its own output inherits the identifier, so vouchers against its leaves keep working. Anyone holding a voucher can redeem it with the CLAIM transition, without the owner and without the operator. CLAIM is two steps in one transaction: the source leaf shrinks by the claim's amount and its counter seq rises by one, then the claim leaf fills a slot proven empty after the first step. The total never changes. Redemption is single-use for free, because the first step changes the source leaf and the signed message names the old one. The caveat is issuance: the covenant prevents redeeming more than the source holds, but nothing prevents its owner from signing more vouchers than that, so a recipient treats a voucher as credit until it is redeemed or the issuer settles it another way. A redemption proves two positions and costs about 1,656 vB at depth 16, more than an exit, so most vouchers are settled off-chain and redemption is the fallback if the issuer stops cooperating.

SPLIT

SPLIT is CLAIM with the authorisation changed: the source leaf's owner signs the spending transaction instead of a voucher. The signature is worthless outside that transaction, so there is no outstanding promise and no credit risk, which suits the common case of a member paying another member and broadcasting the payment themselves. SPLIT still raises the source's seq, so that splitting down and later topping the leaf back up can never restore it byte for byte and revive an old voucher. It needs no opcode for checking signatures on arbitrary messages, so it is the one member-to-member transition that exists in every tier, including MIN.

Channel leaves

A channel leaf is a leaf whose exit key is the funding key of a Lightning channel between the leaf's owner and a counterparty, normally the operator. The channel's funding output does not exist while the leaf is in the tree; it appears as the payout of whatever transaction eventually removes the leaf. Payments between two channel leaves are ordinary HTLC forwards (hash-locked, time-limited payments) through the operator, and nothing touches the tree: no spend, no proof, no fee. A tree of N channel leaves is a Lightning hub with one output on-chain, and because the hash locks and timeouts are the usual ones, the operator can forward between a channel leaf and the public Lightning Network. The price is time: a force close must first get an exit confirmed through the shared output, so HTLC deadlines need about 220 blocks of headroom, against about 40 on the public network. Channel leaves require the FULL, MID or CCV tier, an exact payout and a leaf that no voucher can draw on.

A credit leaf for receiving over Lightning

A user with no on-chain coins can receive into a credit leaf: a leaf the operator funds and owns, whose exit key is the recipient's. Each Lightning payment to the recipient arrives at the operator, which settles the payment first and only then signs a new voucher against the credit leaf for the running total. That costs no on-chain bytes per payment, has no minimum amount and never expires, and the recipient can redeem the latest voucher alone. The order matters: a voucher signed before the payment settled would stay redeemable if the payment then failed, and the operator would have no way to cancel it. The recipient trusts the operator only for the one payment between settling and signing. Whatever the credit leaf holds is the recipient's on every path the operator can take alone, since an exit or an eviction pays the whole leaf to the recipient's key, so the operator funds it with little more than it has vouched for and tops it up as payments arrive. Plain CLAIM would leave a gap, because the operator could sign itself a voucher and drain the leaf first. The variant CLAIM^x closes it by also requiring a signature from the source leaf's exit key, which is the recipient's, for 8 more bytes of script. The arrangement is one-way on purpose: if the recipient could also spend from the leaf, an older and larger voucher would become a stale state, and refuting it would need a contest window, which is what a channel leaf provides. Receiving suits the credit leaf; two-way traffic suits the channel leaf.

Rewriting a leaf on-chain with UPDATE also moves value, at about 1,073 vB at depth 16, ten times a bare spend. UPDATE is for maintenance: resizing a balance, renewing a lease, rotating a key. Payments go through channels and vouchers.
