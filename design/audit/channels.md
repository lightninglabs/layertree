# Channel-leaf audit: ways a channel inside a leaf can lose, lock or grief

*An adversarial pass over `spec/channel-leaves.md` 0.3.1 (Appendix C),
the rules of `spec/layertree.md` 0.11.0 that it binds (5.2, 7.2, 7.4,
7.5, 7.9, 7.10, 9.6, 9.8, 10.3, 10.5, 10.8), `design/receive.md`, the
executable appendix (`tools/layertree_chan.py`, `tools/test_chan.py`,
`script/chan.go`, `msg/chan.go`) and the swap and payment pages of the
site, at `main` b40fe58. The question asked of every channel transaction
was how its interaction with a tree transition, with relay policy, or with
the other leg of a swap lets the counterparty, the operator or a third
party take, freeze or tax funds that the channel's signed state says are
somebody else's. The items of `design/audit/protocol.md` (A1 to A8) and
`design/audit/script.md` are taken as settled and are not repeated, except
where this audit found one of them still open on this base. Every claim
the chain model of `tools/test_chan.py` or the simulator can answer was
run; the probe script is throwaway and not committed, and each finding
says what was run and what the model answered. Policy facts are the ones
`protocol.md` states once: a P2TR output is dust below 330 sat, a standard
transaction may carry at most one dust output, and only as ephemeral dust
in a zero-fee transaction whose package spends it (Bitcoin Core
`policy.cpp`, `ephemeral_policy.cpp`). Section numbers without a prefix
are the base spec's; `C.x` is Appendix C.*

## Ranked findings

### B1. A settlement whose balance or HTLC output is below dust is not relayable, and the reference signs one at state 1 of every single-funded channel

**Claim.** `settle_n` is zero-fee with a 0-value P2A anchor, so any
further output below 330 sat makes it a transaction with two dust
outputs, which no default node relays; the reference `settle_outputs`
trims nothing, `msg/chan.go`'s `StateProposal.Validate` accepts any
split, and C.3.4's "trimming follows BOLT-3 practice" cannot be followed
as written, because BOLT-3 trims into fees and a fee is exactly what the
ephemeral anchor forbids.

**Attacker.** The counterparty, in either direction. It needs no key and
no transaction: it only has to leave the channel in a state whose
settlement is non-standard and then refuse to advance. Model (ii) of
C.5.1 puts every channel there at opening, with `b_V = 0`.

**Path.** The suite's own state 1 is `settle_outputs(C, 0, ...)`, outputs
`[1,000,000, 0, 0]`: a 0-value `to_cp` and a 0-value anchor. The probe
counted dust by Core's rule: two dust outputs at `b_V = 0` and at
`b_V = 329`, one at `b_V = 330`; a 200-sat HTLC in flight gives two as
well. `PreCheckEphemeralTx` admits a dust output only in a zero-fee
transaction, so the 0-value anchor cannot be bought back by paying a fee,
and `IsStandardTx` rejects two dust outputs outright. The owner who
force-closes at that state gets `update_1` confirmed (outputs `[C, 0]`,
one dust output, standard) and then holds `U_1`, from which `settle_1`
cannot be relayed and nothing else can be spent for `csv_delay` blocks or
ever. The counterparty engineers the state by paying the owner down to a
sub-dust balance, or by offering a sub-dust HTLC, and then going quiet.
The same holds for the owner against the counterparty.

**Severity.** Lock at the relay layer of the whole capacity, from `U_n`,
for as long as neither party pays a miner out of band or the two
cooperate on the key path. Free to cause; every single-funded channel
starts in it.

**Existing mitigation.** C.3.4's trimming sentence names the right
practice but not where the trimmed value goes; under a zero-fee rule it
cannot go to fees. `protocol.md` A1 saw the same policy wall from the
input side (a larger funding output). Neither the Python reference nor
the Go messages enforce a floor.

**Fix.** A rule in C.3.4: every settlement output other than the anchor
MUST be at least the P2TR dust threshold; a balance or HTLC below it is
trimmed and its value added to the anchor, which then carries the sum of
the trimmed amounts (0 when nothing is trimmed, an ordinary P2A otherwise).
The update transaction is unaffected (`[C, 0]`). `settle_outputs` and
`StateProposal.Validate` apply the floor; a negative test signs a
`b_V = 329` state and shows the dust count. No script changes.

### B2. The receive flow hands the recipient both the preimage and the voucher, and under `CLAIM^x` the operator can never retire a voucher whose inbound HTLC failed

**Claim.** `design/receive.md` has the recipient draw the preimage for a
Lightning HTLC that pays the operator, and has the operator issue a
hash-locked voucher before it holds the preimage; that is the ordering
9.6.2 rule 1 forbids ("the preimage MUST be generated by the party the
HTLC pays"), and on the trust-free credit leaf the page recommends
(`CLAIM^x`, `UPDATE^x`, `EXIT^=`, no `SPLIT`, `REWRITE_h^x`) the
operator has no way to void the voucher once the HTLC has expired, so
each failed receive is a free option on the operator's leaf.

**Attacker.** Any wallet user with a credit leaf. It needs no capital and
no cooperation.

**Path.** The recipient `R` issues an invoice for hash `H`; the payer's
HTLC reaches the operator `O`; `O` issues `R` a cumulative `CLAIM^hx`
voucher for the new running total under `H`. `R` withholds the preimage.
The inbound HTLC expires and `O` fails it upstream; the payer is
refunded. 9.6.1 tells `O` to retire the voucher by advancing the source
leaf's `seq`, and the credit-leaf configuration removes every way of
doing so: the probe ran a FULL tree with `CLAIM^hx`, `UPDATE^x` and
`EXIT_1^=` and a credit leaf owned by `O` with `R`'s exit key. `O`'s
`UPDATE^x` with its owner and operator signatures and no exit-key
signature: rejected. `O`'s `CLAIM^hx` with a voucher to itself and its
own exit-key signature: rejected. `O`'s `EXIT_1^=` and `O`'s eviction at
the lease: accepted, paying the whole leaf to `R`. `R`'s `CLAIM^hx`
2,000 blocks after issuance, with the preimage only `R` ever held:
accepted. A single redemption costs `R` about 1,650 vB; folded into the
next honest cumulative voucher it costs nothing, since the running total
already includes the failed amount and the latest voucher is the one `R`
redeems.

**Severity.** Loss for the operator, bounded by the sum of failed
receives it has issued vouchers for; repeatable. It arises from following
`design/receive.md` and 10.3 together.

**Existing mitigation.** 9.6.2 rule 1 would prevent it, and receive.md
claims to apply "the roles that 9.6.2 requires" while inverting rule 1.
9.6.1's retirement rule assumes the issuer can advance `seq`, which the
credit-leaf configuration is designed to deny. The site's payment page
repeats the receive flow.

**Fix.** Text in `design/receive.md`, 10.3 and the site: in the receive
flow the operator MUST obtain the preimage and settle the inbound HTLC
before it issues or reissues the cumulative voucher, which is then a
plain `CLAIM^x` voucher; the hash-locked form stays with the 9.6.2
ordering, where the issuer holds the preimage. The recipient's exposure
becomes the operator's honesty for one in-flight payment, which is the
"provider that stops issuing" exposure the page already accepts. The
alternative that keeps the hash lock is a new transition that lets the
owner alone advance a credit leaf's `seq` after a height, which is an
`UPDATE` variant with a `CLTV` and the exit-key check dropped, about 10
bytes, visible in `m`; it reintroduces a deadline the holder must redeem
before, so it buys little over the text fix.

### B3. The operator's unissued capacity in a credit leaf belongs to the recipient on every unilateral path

**Claim.** A dedicated credit leaf (10.3, `design/receive.md`) is owned
by the operator with the recipient's `exit_key`, in a tree where every
transition that could change it needs that key; the operator's own
removals pay the whole leaf to the recipient, so the part of the leaf the
operator has not issued vouchers against is recoverable only with the
recipient's consent, and receive.md's analogy to an LSP funding an
inbound channel is wrong in the one respect that matters.

**Attacker.** The recipient, by doing nothing.

**Path.** The probe's credit leaf holds 1,000,000 sat with one voucher of
40,000 outstanding. The operator's `EXIT_1^=` pays 1,000,000 to
`P2TR(recipient exit key)`; its eviction at the lease pays the same; its
`UPDATE^x` down to 1 sat is rejected without the recipient's exit-key
signature and accepted with it. The 960,000 sat the operator never issued
can therefore leave the leaf only by an `UPDATE^x` the recipient
co-signs, and a recipient who prefers to wait gets all of it on any
operator-initiated removal. An operator that sizes credit leaves the way
an LSP sizes inbound channels has given each recipient its allocation
outright.

**Severity.** Lock of the operator's unissued capital per credit leaf,
convertible to loss by the recipient at any time the operator needs the
leaf gone; a hostage position the recipient can price.

**Existing mitigation.** 10.3 states the property ("can only grow or be
paid to its recipient") as the guarantee the recipient wants, and
receive.md tells the operator to fund "the inbound capacity it is willing
to extend". Nothing says the operator cannot take it back. The 7.9 rule 3
exemption lets the operator top up a credit leaf by amount-only
`REWRITE_h^x` without any signature, which is the honest way to keep the
leaf small.

**Fix.** Text in 10.3 and `design/receive.md`: a credit leaf's whole
amount is the recipient's on every unilateral path, so the operator MUST
fund it with no more than it is prepared to treat as already paid and
SHOULD top it up by amount-only `REWRITE_h^x` (about 117 vB per
position) as credit accrues, rather than pre-funding an allocation.
Receive.md's cost table should carry that top-up as the per-batch cost of
the trust-free configuration. The structural alternative is a leaf whose
exit pays the operator the unissued remainder, which is a channel leaf.

### B4. A counterparty with stale states can delay the settlement one block per state, and the C.4.4 budget has no term for it

**Claim.** The budget of C.4.4 counts one block for the update, but an
`update_m` can be bound to `U_j` for any `j < m`, so a counterparty
holding states `1..k` can publish them in sequence, each on the previous
one's output, and the honest `update_n` lands only after the last; the
two blocks of margin C.4.4 measures absorb two stale states and no more.

**Attacker.** The HTLC offerer, holding the channel's history of signed
states, which it does by construction (C.3.6 has both parties retain
every state they signed, and nothing deletes the old ones).

**Path.** The probe ran the deadline scenario of `test_deadlines` with
the counterparty publishing `k` stale updates before the honest one. At
the spec bound (`delta = 221`): `k = 2`, the payee's sweep lands in block
`cltv_expiry` and is safe; `k = 3`, it lands one block after
`cltv_expiry` and the offerer's timeout sweep is final first, so the
claim becomes a fee race or a loss. At the exact budget (`delta = 219`),
`k = 1` suffices. Each stale update is 153 vB bound to the funding
output or 161 vB bound to `U_j`, plus a 152 vB anchor child, about 313 vB
per block of delay; at 10 sat/vB that is about 3,100 sat per block and
about 690,000 sat to push a close past a 220-block budget. TRUC
serialises the rounds: a v3 transaction may have one unconfirmed
ancestor, so the honest `update_n` cannot be chained on an unconfirmed
stale update together with its own anchor child, and each round costs a
block. In the mempool the honest party can replace a pending stale update
by fee, which turns the delay into an auction the attacker must win every
block, at a cost it chooses; C.9.6 states TRUC's bound on anchor-child
pinning and nothing about this.

**Severity.** Grief with a payoff: the attacker pays about 313 vB per
block to deny an HTLC worth whatever the channel carries, and recovers
the HTLC if it wins the final race. Medium: the honest party can always
outbid in the mempool, which makes it the `beta` of 10.5 on the channel's
own output chain, but the budget does not say so and the payee has no
instruction to reserve fees in proportion to the HTLCs it accepts.

**Existing mitigation.** C.3.3's "restarts `csv_delay`" is true and is
not the point; the clock that matters is `cltv_expiry`. C.4.4's margin is
two blocks. C.5.5's fee reserve is for the party's own chain, not for
outbidding stale states.

**Fix.** Text in C.4.4 and C.9.6: the budget's update term is
`1 + beta_chan` blocks, where `beta_chan` is the number of stale updates
the counterparty gets confirmed ahead of the honest one, bounded by fee
as in 10.5's assumption [C]; a party SHOULD hold a fee reserve
proportional to the HTLC value it has in flight and SHOULD replace a
pending stale update rather than wait for it. The test: the probe's
`k = 3` case as a documented failure of the spec bound.

### B5. `REWRITE_h`'s amount-only exemption still applies to `SEQ_FINAL` leaves on this base: A1's fix is in the Lean model only

**Claim.** `protocol.md` A1 is recorded as "fix in flight (script rule,
spec 0.11.0)" in `design/review.md` F22 and `brain.md`; the spec header
already reads 0.11.0 (for the MID `NONNEG` fix) and
`formal/LayerTree/Cohort.lean` already excludes `old.seq = π.seqFinal`
from the exemption, so a reader of the version or the formal model would
take it as landed, but 7.9 rule 3 in `spec/layertree.md` reads as before
and `_rewrite_step` in `tools/layertree_scripts.py` compares `new[8:]`
with `old[8:]` and the amounts and nothing else, so the operator can
still raise a channel leaf's capacity alone.

**Attacker.** The operator, as in A1.

**Path.** The probe built `REWRITE_2` at `D = 4` over a subtree holding
one channel leaf (`seq = 0xFFFFFFFF`, evictor the operator) and rewrote
it to the same leaf with `amount + 1`, funded from the fee input, with
zero owner signatures in the witness: accepted. The consequences are
A1's: `update_n` bound to a `C + 1` output pays a 1-sat fee with a
0-value anchor and is rejected as dust by every default node.

**Severity.** A1's: relay-layer lock of every channel in a cohort at one
transaction and 1 sat per leaf. Listed here because the version header
and the formal model say it is closed and the spec text and scripts say
it is open; the 0.11.0 label should not ship with the rule half landed.

**Existing mitigation.** The Lean theorem (`Cohort.lean`, the `seqFinal`
hypotheses) and the 5.2 marker. Nothing in script.

**Fix.** A1's: one `OP_SUBSTR` of the `seq` field, one `OP_EQUAL` against
the tier's `SEQ_FINAL` and an `OP_IF` inside the exemption branch of
`_rewrite_step` (about 12 bytes per position), the matching sentence in
7.9 rule 3, the Go mirror, regenerated golden vectors, and the probe as a
negative in `test_rewrite`. The `brain.md` and `design/review.md` entries
should say which layers the fix has reached.

### B6. `chan_id` binds `m`, which two trees can share and which a detach changes; `tree_id` is the identifier the rest of the spec moved to

**Claim.** C.2.2 says binding `m` means "a state signed for one tree
cannot be replayed in another", but 6.2 says `m` names a rule set that
every tree with the same parameters shares unless a `CLAIM` script
carries `tree_id`, and C.6.5 offers omitting `CLAIM` as one of the two
ways to make a tree channel-safe; a `DETACH_h` moves the leaf under
`m_h`, so the identifier the channel scripts carry stops matching the
tree the leaf is in.

**Attacker.** None found. Two trees with the same `m` can hold
byte-identical channel leaves only if someone funds the second, which is
the duplicate-leaf case `protocol.md` rejected: the same states bind to
both funding outputs and the funder pays. The finding is that the
binding C.2.2 claims is weaker than stated and that the counterparty's
"verify from `m`" checks of C.9.7 and the `chan_id` diverge after a
detach, with `script/chan.go` already naming the field `TreeID` and
documenting it as `m`.

**Severity.** Low; a specification claim that does not hold as written,
and an identifier that is not stable across the lineage.

**Existing mitigation.** C.9.1's uniqueness rules on `P_chan` and
`exit_key`, and 5.2's `(owner_key, nonce)` rule, carry the binding in
practice.

**Fix.** Rule change in C.2.2: `chan_id` binds `tree_id` (6.2) in place
of `m`, for the reason 7.5 gives for vouchers; it is unique per lineage
by construction and survives `DETACH_h`. Zero bytes of script; the Python
and Go derivations, the `chan` golden vectors and the `ChanOpen` message
change. C.9.7's "verifiable from `m`" stays, since the option set is
what `m` names.

### B7. Two statements a counterparty would rely on are wrong: `owner_key` compromise in a lenient tree, and `CLAIM` in a CCV tree

**Claim.** C.6.2 says a compromise of `owner_key` "costs the user an
unwanted force-close". In a tree without `EXIT^=`, 7.2 rule 3 lets the
owner's exit pay any amount down to zero to the funding output, and the
script audit confirmed a zero payout is accepted, so the hot key takes the
whole capacity, the counterparty's balance included. C.7's CCV row says a
CCV tree "has no `CLAIM` (C.6.5 does not arise)", and 4.6 says `CLAIM`
and `CLAIM^h` are CCV scripts since 0.10.0, so a counterparty reading
C.7 would skip the `SEQ_FINAL` check in exactly the tree where a plain
voucher against the channel leaf drains it.

**Attacker.** Whoever holds a stolen `owner_key`, in a lenient tree; the
owner, in a CCV tree whose counterparty trusted C.7.

**Path.** By the rules cited; no probe needed beyond the script audit's
zero-payout case.

**Severity.** Low as text, since C.6.1 already makes the lenient tree a
"trust the owner with everything" configuration and 4.5's table carries
the CCV `SEQ_FINAL` values; the sentences are the ones a reader checks.

**Fix.** C.6.2: say the force-close bound holds under `EXIT^=` and that in
a lenient tree the hot key is a full-balance key, which is a further
reason to make `EXIT^=` a MUST for channel-hosting trees rather than the
SHOULD of 7.2 rule 6 and 9.8. C.7: the CCV row applies C.6.5 as FULL
does.

## Considered and rejected

Each item names the claim examined and the reason it does not stand, so
the owner need not re-examine it.

* **A second funding output created by paying `C` to `P2TR(exit_key)`.**
  Anyone can, since the address is public once the leaf exits. Every
  signed state binds to it and pays the two parties again from the
  donor's money; `NUM_INPUTS = 1` stops the half-spend. The same holds
  for an operator grafting a byte-identical channel leaf, which
  `protocol.md` already rejected.
* **The last-signature free option of C.3.6.** After both settle
  signatures, the party that receives the other's `update_{n+1}` partial
  first can choose between `n` and `n+1`. That is the asymmetry every
  channel protocol has at the moment a state is half-signed; the HTLC
  outputs of state `n` are what make a withheld fulfilment claimable
  on-chain, as in BOLT-3.
* **Anchor-child pinning of `update_n` or `settle_n`.** The P2A anchor is
  keyless, so the counterparty can attach the first child, but TRUC limits
  a v3 parent to one child of at most 1,000 vB and the child is replaced
  by sibling eviction; a high-fee junk child confirms the parent, which is
  what the honest party wanted. C.9.6 is right about this; the surface it
  misses is B4.
* **A reorganisation of the exit or the update.** The channel's signed
  states rebind to whatever outpoint the funding output comes back at,
  and the exit is rebuilt against the new root if a conflicting transition
  took its place. What a reorg does cost is the `csv_delay` clock and the
  `G_exit` already spent, against a C.4.4 margin of two blocks; a
  one-block reorg that drops the update consumes half of it. Not an attack
  anyone can cause at will; C.4.4's `G_sweep` is the place to carry a
  reorg allowance if the owner wants one.
* **Stale-state publication while the honest party is offline.** With
  `evict_after = 0` the counterparty picks the moment, evicts, and
  publishes a stale state; the honest party or its tower must answer
  within `csv_delay`. This is the update machine's stated one-sided
  failure mode (C.9.2, C.9.3) and the same exposure as any channel.
* **`evict_after` absent from `chan_id`.** Intended (C.6.7) so that a lease
  renewal leaves every state valid; `evict_after` is a deferral of the
  evictor's right and changing it needs the owner's signature under every
  transition.
* **Splice races.** Between signing the `UPDATE` of C.5.2 and its
  confirmation the owner can still `EXIT` the old leaf, which materialises
  the old capacity under the old states while the operator's splice-in
  input stays unspent; every outcome is consistent with what each party
  contributed. Old states cannot reach the new funding output, since the
  leaf bytes that carried the old `exit_key` no longer exist.
* **Witness malleation of channel transactions.** The witness is one
  signature, the script and the control block; nothing in it is
  unsigned, and the txid does not cover it.
* **A v2 or key-path spend of the funding output to escape TRUC.** The
  update signature commits `nVersion`, tested as a negative; the key path
  is the cooperative close.
* **`cltv_expiry` racing the timeout at expiry.** After `cltv_expiry`
  both HTLC paths are live and the preimage holder competes by fee; that
  is the ordinary HTLC race and C.4.4's budget is what keeps the payee
  ahead of it, subject to B4.
* **The offerer's deadline in C.5.6.** The party that offered an HTLC the
  payee does not resolve must start its force-close `G_exit + 1 + csv_delay
  + 1` blocks before `cltv_expiry` to time out promptly; started later, it
  learns the preimage up to 220 blocks after expiry and needs the upstream
  delta to be twice the budget. C.5.6's "approaching that bound MUST
  force-close" is the right rule; it should name the offerer's deadline
  as well as the payee's.
* **`SPLIT`, `CLAIM`, `CLAIM^x` against a channel leaf.** All compute
  `seq + 1` and fail on `SEQ_FINAL`; the Lean theorem `claim_not_final`
  and the FULL and MID suites cover both marker values. The one gap is
  the marker of the wrong tier in a tree of the other, which `test_scripts`
  already checks.
* **`DETACH_h` and cohort sweeps changing a channel leaf.** A detach
  keeps the bytes and the option set (7.10 rule 3); a sweep removes the
  leaf and pays it exactly (7.7 rule 2). Neither changes a channel leaf's
  bytes. `UPDATE` and `REWRITE_h` need the owner and the operator, who are
  the two parties when the counterparty is the operator; for user-to-user
  leaves C.6.6 and A3 already require the `^x` forms. B5 is the only
  remaining path.
* **Operator contention on the tree output during the exit.** C.9.5's
  `beta` and 10.5's assumption [C] already state it, and the budget
  carries `G_exit`.
* **MID's seven-HTLC limit and `0xC0` encodings.** Tested; `evict_after
  = 0` under MID numerics is a leaf `test_mid` already uses.
* **A `JOIN` of a channel leaf before state 1 is signed.** C.5.1 step 4
  covers the party that funds; an operator-funded leaf with no state is
  the operator's own money stuck behind a 2-of-2.
* **Items already in the prior audits, not re-examined:** A1's lock
  (except its status, B5), the descendant and cluster limits on the exit's
  output 0, TRUC being unavailable to transitions, the `CLAIM^h` issuer
  race of 9.6.2 rule 3, eviction voiding vouchers and the lease check,
  `NUMS` credit leaves as squat tokens, and the 220-block budget's
  dependence on `N`.

## Rules of C.6 and C.9 without a test in `tools/test_chan.py`

The suite covers C.6.1 (a funding output short of `C` carries no state;
`EXIT_1^=` creates exactly `C`), C.2.3, C.3.5, C.4.4, C.5.6 (an eviction
with an HTLC in flight), C.7's collation limit and the contested close.
These rules have no case:

* C.6.2: `owner_key` distinct from `exit_key`, `U_chan`, `V_chan`; no
  negative, and no case for the lenient-tree consequence of B7.
* C.6.3: `evictor_key = V_evict`, not `NUMS`; no case shows a
  `NUMS`-evictor channel leaf's force-close failing.
* C.6.4: a channel leaf with a future `evict_after` cannot be evicted
  before it, and an HTLC expiring before `evict_after + budget` is
  unenforceable; the suite only evicts at `evict_after = 0`.
  `HtlcAdd.CheckDeadline` in `msg/chan.go` ignores `evict_after`.
* C.6.5: no `CLAIM` or `SPLIT` is attempted against the channel leaf in
  this suite (the marker is covered in `test_scripts`).
* C.6.6: no user-to-user leaf, no `UPDATE^x` host, no demonstration of the
  owner-plus-operator splice-down that plain `UPDATE` permits.
* C.6.7: no positive case that an `UPDATE` renewing `evict_after` leaves
  `update_n` bindable (by construction, since `chan_id` omits it, but
  untested).
* C.9.1: `chan_id` is tested for `m`, capacity, epoch and nonce; not for
  `owner_key` or `csv_delay`.
* C.9.5, C.9.6: contention and fees are not modelled; `G_exit` is an
  `advance`, anchor children do not exist, and no stale-state spam (B4)
  is run.
* C.9.7: no check of the option set from `m`.
* C.5.2: no on-chain epoch transition (an `UPDATE` to a new `exit_key`
  followed by an old-epoch update failing against the new funding
  output).
* 9.6.2 rule 5: the suite's honest swap uses a source leaf with
  `evict_after = 0` and the operator as evictor, which is the voucher rule
  5 now tells the holder to refuse; the rule has no case.
* C.3.4 trimming and B1's dust floor: no case; `settle_outputs` signs
  the non-standard state.
* C.7's MID rule that a party MUST NOT sign a state whose collation
  exceeds 488 bytes is tested in the simulator but not enforced by
  `StateProposal.Validate`.
